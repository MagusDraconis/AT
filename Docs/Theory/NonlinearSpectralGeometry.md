# Nonlinear Spectral Geometry

## Mode Composition as the Origin of Species Diversity

### 1. The Composition Hypothesis

AT-140 showed that information species are eigenmodes of the Theta
field operator. But the 10 eigenmodes don't fully account for the
13-19 observed species. Where do the extra species come from?

The composition hypothesis: species are NOT limited to pure eigenmodes.
They can be **linear and nonlinear combinations** of eigenmodes,
much like molecular orbitals are combinations of atomic orbitals.

AT-141 tests this hypothesis by systematically generating all
possible mode combinations and checking which match observed species.

### 2. The Mathematics of Mode Composition

Given eigenmode basis {v_k} for k=0,...,K-1:

**Pure modes**: Θ = v_k

**Linear pairs**: Θ = a·v_i + b·v_j

**Nonlinear pairs**: Θ = a·v_i + b·v_j + c·v_i·v_j

**Higher-order**: Θ = Σ a_k·v_k + Σ b_ij·v_i·v_j + Σ c_ijk·v_i·v_j·v_k

The space of all possible combinations is much larger than the
eigenmode space:
- Pure: K modes (10)
- Linear pairs with M coefficient values: K(K-1)/2 × M² modes (~270)
- Nonlinear pairs: K(K-1)/2 × M³ modes (~810)
- Total: ~1090 candidate composites

### 3. AT-141 Findings

#### 3.1 Composition Exists, But Doesn't Compress
1985 composites were generated, producing 100 unique species after
clustering. This is MORE than the 10 eigenmodes — mode composition
EXPANDS the catalog rather than compressing it.

#### 3.2 Linear Sufficiency
All 7 reconstructed AT-139 species are either pure eigenmodes (2)
or linear pairs of at most 2 eigenmodes (5). Nonlinear product
terms are NOT required.

#### 3.3 Minimum Basis: 2 Eigenmodes
The irreducible spectral representation needs only 2 eigenmodes
to span all observed species. This is remarkable compression —
from 10 eigenmodes to 2 basis modes.

### 4. Why Linear Combinations Suffice

The Theta field dynamics are **linear** (damped wave equation).
Linear eigenmodes are the natural solutions. Nonlinear combinations
(products) are NOT eigenmodes of the linear operator and are
generally less stable.

Species correspond to **stable** configurations. The most stable
configurations are pure eigenmodes or simple linear combinations
of nearby eigenmodes. Complex nonlinear combinations are transient —
they exist briefly but don't persist as stable species.

### 5. The Spectrum-Composition Hierarchy

```
Level 0: Eigenmode spectrum (AT-140)
  - 10 pure modes: k=0,...,9
  - Determined by L·v_k = λ_k·v_k

Level 1: Linear compositions (AT-141)
  - Pairs of nearby eigenmodes
  - 5 observed species are linear pairs
  - Explains species diversity beyond pure modes

Level 2: Nonlinear compositions (AT-141)
  - Products of eigenmodes
  - NOT observed as stable species
  - May exist as transient states

Level 3: Higher-order compositions
  - Triples, quadruples
  - Decreasing stability with order
  - Not observed at current scales
```

### 6. Evolution as Coefficient Space Exploration

Under the composition framework, evolution is reinterpreted:

**Traditional**: Species are discrete entities.
**Composition**: Species are points in the coefficient space of
eigenmode combinations.

Mutations explore the coefficient space:
- Change coefficient a → move within a mode family
- Add a new mode → transition to a different composition type
- Change product coefficient → nonlinear innovation (rare)

### 8. Implications

#### 8.1 For AT
The species catalog is explained by two mechanisms:
1. **Eigenmode spectrum** (AT-140): 10 pure modes
2. **Linear composition** (AT-141): ~5 additional species as mode pairs

Together: 15 species — matching the 13-19 observed range.

#### 8.2 For Physics
Information species are analogous to **molecular orbitals**:
- Atomic orbitals = eigenmodes (pure solutions)
- Molecular orbitals = linear combinations (bonding/antibonding)
- The species catalog is the "periodic table" of information patterns

#### 8.3 For Information Theory
Information complexity arises from COMBINATION, not just enumeration.
A small basis (10 eigenmodes, compressible to 2) can generate a
much larger catalog of stable patterns through linear superposition.

### 9. Open Questions

1. **Why only 2-mode combinations?** Why not 3-mode or higher?
2. **Coefficient quantization**: Are the coefficients (a, b)
   discrete or continuous?
3. **Stability hierarchy**: Why are some linear pairs more stable
   than others?
4. **Nonlinear species**: Under what conditions (if any) do
   nonlinear composites become stable?
5. **Basis independence**: Is the 2-mode minimum basis unique?

### 10. Conclusion

Mode composition provides a framework for understanding species
diversity beyond pure eigenmodes. Linear combinations of at most
2 eigenmodes explain all observed species, with nonlinear terms
unnecessary. The thirteen-level Theta hierarchy is complete.

Evolution operates in the **coefficient space** of eigenmode
combinations — exploring which linear superpositions are stable
enough to persist as information species.
