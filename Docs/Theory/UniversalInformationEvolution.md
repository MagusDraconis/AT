# Universal Information Evolution

## Darwinian Dynamics as an Inevitable Emergent Phenomenon

### 1. The Universality Claim

Darwinian information evolution in the Theta layer is **universal** —
it does not depend on the specific fitness function w = r/c.
Any system with reproduction, resource constraints, and property
asymmetry will exhibit selection, competition, and extinction.

This was demonstrated by AT-137: 147 runs across 7 alternative
fitness models and 7 resource regimes. Evolution persisted at
100% selection robustness and 85% evolution persistence score.

### 2. The Minimal Conditions for Evolution

AT-137 identifies three minimal ingredients for Darwinian
information evolution:

#### 2.1 Reproduction (INEVITABLE)
Entities must be able to make copies of themselves.
In Theta: species reproduce through field-mediated template copying
(AT-134). Reproduction rate r > 0 is sufficient.

#### 2.2 Constraints (INEVITABLE)
Resources must be finite. Infinite resources = no competition.
In Theta: six resource budgets limit the information ecology
(AT-135). Any finite capacity K creates carrying capacity.

#### 2.3 Asymmetry (CONTINGENT)
Entities must differ in their properties. If all are identical,
there can be no differential survival.
In Theta: species differ in reproduction rate r and resource
consumption c (AT-133). These differences create fitness
asymmetry.

### 3. The Emergence Theorem

Given (1) reproduction, (2) constraints, and (3) asymmetry:

**Theorem**: The system will exhibit Darwinian dynamics:
- Differential population growth (selection)
- Population crashes when constraints bind (extinction)
- Competitive exclusion when one entity is more efficient (competition)
- Stable coexistence when entities occupy different niches (coexistence)

**Proof sketch**:
1. Each entity i has population N_i, reproduction r_i, consumption c_i.
2. Total consumption C = Σ(c_i · N_i) must satisfy C ≤ K (constraint).
3. When C > K, some individuals die (resource pressure).
4. Entities with lower c_i survive longer (higher effective carrying capacity).
5. Entities with higher r_i produce more offspring (higher growth rate).
6. The ratio r_i/c_i determines which entities dominate.
7. BUT any monotonic function of (r_i, c_i) that ranks entities differently
   will also produce differential survival — just with a different ranking.

**The fitness function is not unique — the PHENOMENON of selection is.**

### 4. Why r/c Still Matters

While evolution is universal, w = r/c remains the OPTIMAL fitness
function for Theta because:

1. **Parameter-free**: No fitted constants.
2. **Derivable**: Emerges from dN/dt = r·N·(1 - c·N/K).
3. **Predictive**: Spearman ρ = 1.000 (AT-136).
4. **Interpretable**: Clear physical meaning — offspring per resource unit.

Other fitness functions (r²/c, r/c², etc.) also produce evolution,
but with different species rankings and different predictive power.

### 5. The Robustness Hierarchy

Not all Darwinian signatures are equally robust:

| Signature | Universality | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Extinctions | 98% | Most universal — any constraint produces crashes |
| Selection | 100% | Universal but detection threshold needs calibration |
| Competition | 98% | Nearly universal under constraints |
| Species ranking | 57% (τ) | Moderately stable — depends on fitness model |
| Coexistence | 3% | Least universal — requires specific conditions |
| Specific fitness values | 0% | NOT universal — absolute fitness is model-dependent |

The **structure** of evolution (selection, extinction, competition)
is universal. The **details** (which species wins, by how much)
are model-dependent.

### 6. The Hidden Invariant

Beneath the specific fitness law w = r/c lies a deeper invariant:

> **RESOURCE CONSTRAINT × REPRODUCTION → ECOLOGICAL DYNAMICS**

This is the "engine" of evolution in Theta. The specific fitness
function is the "fuel gauge" — it tells you who wins. But the
engine runs regardless of how you calibrate the gauge.

The invariant can be expressed as a causal chain:

```
Finite resources
    ↓
Resource pressure (P = C/K)
    ↓
Differential carrying capacity (K_i = K/c_i)
    ↓
Differential survival (species differ in c_i)
    ↓
Differential reproduction (species differ in r_i)
    ↓
Selection, competition, extinction
```

Every step in this chain is ROBUST to changes in fitness definition.
Only the final step — "who wins?" — depends on the fitness model.

dynamics are substrate-independent. Evolution does not require
DNA, cells, or organisms — only replicators, constraints, and
variation.
