### 3. Mathematical Framework

#### 3.1 Resource-Constrained Growth

dN_i/dt = r_i · N_i · f(R)

where:
- r_i = intrinsic growth rate of species i
- N_i = population of species i
- f(R) = resource availability function

Resource pressure:
P = Σ(c_i · N_i) / K

where:
- c_i = resource consumption per individual of species i
- K = total resource capacity

#### 3.2 Fitness Under Constraints

w_i = r_i / c_i

where:
- w_i = fitness of species i
- r_i = reproduction rate
- c_i = total resource consumption per individual

This is the fundamental fitness metric for information species:
fitness = reproduction per unit resource consumed.

Species A: w_A = 0.080 / 5.8 = **0.0138** (highest)
Species D: w_D = 0.120 / 19.0 = **0.0063** (2.2× lower)

Despite having 1.5× the reproduction rate, D's fitness is less than
half of A's — because D consumes 3.3× more resources.

#### 3.3 Selection Coefficient

s_i = (dN_i/dt) / (r_i · N_i)

When s_i > 0: species is favored (growing faster than intrinsic rate).
When s_i < 0: species is disfavored (resource constraints suppress growth).

For AT-135:
- s_A = +0.009 (mildly favored)
- s_D = -0.041 (strongly disfavored)

#### 3.4 Extinction Probability

P_ext(i) depends on:
1. Population size N_i (smaller → higher risk)
2. Resource pressure P (higher → higher risk)
3. Competitive pressure from other species

Stochastic extinction threshold: N_i < 2 with P_ext ≈ 1/N_i.

#### 3.5 Coexistence Conditions

Two species can coexist when:
α_ij < K_i / K_j

where α_ij is the competition coefficient (how much species j
reduces species i's carrying capacity).

For AT-135: A and B coexist when resource capacity is sufficient
for both to maintain viable populations.

### 4. Physical Interpretation

#### 4.1 Why Does Selection Emerge?

Selection is not an added feature — it's a CONSEQUENCE of:

1. **Finite resources**: The Theta field has limited total amplitude,
   memory, coherence, lifetime, spatial nodes, and bandwidth.

2. **Differential consumption**: Species with complex patterns (D)
   consume more resources than simple patterns (A).

3. **Heritable efficiency**: Offspring inherit pattern complexity
   from parents (H=0.786), so resource efficiency is heritable.

4. **Limited carrying capacity**: When total consumption exceeds
   capacity, some individuals must die.

The mathematical structure is:
Pattern complexity → Resource consumption → Carrying capacity → Selection

#### 4.2 The Selection Mechanism

```
HIGH resource pressure:
  → Birth rates suppressed (less energy for reproduction)
  → Death rates increased (resource stress)
  → High-consumption species hit carrying capacity first
  → Low-consumption species have spare capacity
  → Low-consumption species outcompete high-consumption species
  → Extinction of least efficient species
```

#### 4.3 Why Not a Simple Replicator?

The replicator equation (dx_i/dt = x_i·(f_i - ⟨f⟩)) assumes:
- Infinite population (no stochastic effects)
- Constant fitness (no resource feedback)
- No mutation during selection
- Instantaneous fitness response

AT-135 violates all four assumptions:
- Finite populations (10-500 individuals) → stochastic noise
- Resource-dependent fitness (changes with population) → feedback
- Ongoing mutation (μ=0.214/gen) → traits change during selection
- Delayed fitness response (resource regeneration) → non-equilibrium

This is why the replicator fit is "None" — not because selection is absent,
but because the dynamics are RICHER than the simplest model.
