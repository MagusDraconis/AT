# Spectral Information Theory

## Eigenmodes as the Foundation of Information Species

### 1. The Spectral Hypothesis

Information species in the Theta field are not arbitrary attractors —
they are **eigenmodes** of the discrete Theta field operator.

This hypothesis transforms our understanding:
- Species are NOT discovered by evolution — they PRE-EXIST as eigenmodes
- Evolution is a SEARCH process over a pre-existing spectral landscape
- Species properties are DETERMINED by the eigenvalue problem
- Innovation saturation is INEVITABLE — the spectrum is finite

### 2. The Theta Eigenvalue Problem

The discrete Theta field on N points with damping γ:

**L · v_k = λ_k · v_k**

where L = -(1/Δx²) · D² - γ · I

The solutions are analytic:
- v_k[n] = sin(π(k+1)(n+1)/(N+1))
- λ_k = -4(N+1)² · sin²(π(k+1)/(2(N+1))) - γ

These are the **natural oscillation modes** of the field.
Each mode has:
- A characteristic frequency ω_k = √(|λ_k + γ|)
- A damping rate γ (same for all modes)
- A nodal structure (k zero crossings)
- A symmetry class (odd/even by k parity)

### 3. The Spectral Landscape

The spectral landscape is the set of all stable eigenmodes:

```
k=0: Uniform mode         — species A (TQM-133)
k=1: Fundamental mode     — species B (standing wave)
k=2: 2nd harmonic         — species with 2 nodes
k=3: 3rd harmonic         — species with 3 nodes
...
k=9: 9th harmonic         — highest stable mode
```

Each mode is a **candidate information species**.
The mode's properties determine the species' characteristics:
- Nodal count → complexity (TQM-138)
- Frequency → oscillation rate
- Stability → persistence (TQM-130)
- Mode order → connectivity (TQM-139)

### 4. Why the Spectrum is Finite

The spectrum is finite because:
1. The field is **discrete** (N points) → N eigenmodes
2. High-k modes have **high frequency** → more damping loss
3. Beyond a cutoff, modes are **unstable** → cannot persist

For N=10 and γ=0.1, all 10 modes are stable. For larger N
or higher γ, high-k modes become unstable → fewer species.

The finite spectrum explains:
- **Innovation saturation** (TQM-138): when all stable modes
  are discovered, no new species can emerge
- **Species count** (TQM-139): ~10-19 species = number of
  stable eigenmodes in the relevant N range
- **Bounded complexity** (TQM-138): maximum nodal count =
  maximum stable mode order

### 5. Mode Mixing and Evolutionary Transitions

Transitions between species correspond to **mode mixing**:

When two eigenmodes coexist in the field, they produce
**beating patterns** — superpositions that oscillate between
the two mode shapes. This is the mechanism of evolutionary
transition:

Species A (k=0) + perturbation → beating with k=1 mode →
transition to Species B (k=1)

The transition probability depends on:
- Mode overlap: higher overlap → easier transition
- Frequency difference: similar frequencies → resonant mixing
- Energy barrier: damping loss during transition

### 6. Spectral Predictions

The spectral theory makes testable predictions:

1. **Species count**: = number of stable eigenmodes (depends on N, γ)
2. **Species patterns**: = eigenvectors v_k[n]
3. **Species frequencies**: determined by eigenvalues λ_k
4. **Innovation capacity**: = total number of eigenmodes
5. **Forbidden patterns**: = spectral gaps (no eigenmodes available)
6. **Transition pathways**: = mode adjacency in k-space
7. **Hub species**: = low-k modes (broad, smooth, many connections)
8. **Bottleneck species**: = high-k modes (narrow, complex, few connections)

### 7. Evolution as Spectral Search

Under the spectral theory, evolution is re-interpreted:

**Traditional view**: Evolution CREATES new species through
mutation + selection over time.

**Spectral view**: Evolution DISCOVERS pre-existing eigenmodes
through exploration of pattern space. The species already exist
as solutions to the eigenvalue problem; evolution merely finds them.

This explains:
- Why innovation is bounded (finite spectrum)
- Why species are reproducible (same eigenmodes across runs)
- Why complexity has limits (maximum stable mode order)
- Why some patterns are impossible (spectral gaps)

waves of the Theta field, analogous to:
- Atomic orbitals (solutions of Schrödinger equation)
- Vibrational modes of a string (solutions of wave equation)
- Electromagnetic cavity modes (solutions of Maxwell's equations)

The discreteness of species is a **quantization effect** — the
finite spatial domain (N points) imposes boundary conditions
that quantize the allowed modes.

Information has a **spectral structure**. Not all patterns are
possible — only those that are eigenmodes of the information
medium can persist. This imposes fundamental limits on:
- Information complexity (maximum mode order)
- Information diversity (number of eigenmodes)
- Information innovation (finite spectrum)
