Combined with TQM-012 (spatial merger at close range), this suggests

Minimal Identity Formation (280 runs: 14 histories × 20 seeds).

- Amplitude variations (mean 0.061) produce larger shifts than
  timing variations (mean 0.040)
- Multi-pulse (mean 0.081) produces larger shifts than timing
- 3-pulse (most aggressive variant) only reaches 0.133 distance

Key insight: IDENTITY IS STOCHASTICALLY DOMINATED. The variation between

This means: for small perturbations, you CANNOT reliably distinguish
a changed history from a different random seed. Identity formation is
dominated by initial conditions, not fine historical details.

Complementary to TQM-044 (AB ≠ BA for LARGE history differences),
TQM-051 shows that SMALL historical differences do NOT create
distinguishable identities. Identity has a "resolution limit" —
perturbations below this limit are indistinguishable from noise.

Identity has FIVE established properties:
- STATIC STABILITY (TQM-047): Identity ≠ energy across runs.
- BAND STABILITY (TQM-048): Identity survives ±25% energy changes.
- LATENT STABILITY (TQM-049): Identity recovers after energy collapse.
- IDENTITY EXCLUSION (TQM-050): Identities repel at close range.
- RESOLUTION LIMIT (TQM-051): Small perturbations indistinguishable from noise.
  Identity requires a minimum historical difference to be distinguishable.
  Below this resolution, identity is dominated by stochastic initial conditions.

condensates together, but this motion INCREASES resonance error —
oscillators are pulled out of locally-optimal phase configurations.
Error is a CONSEQUENCE of motion, not its CAUSE. The system does
not naturally minimize resonance error (consistent with TQM-054:
no global minimization principle).

The causal chain is: coupling forces → spatial motion → error increase.
Not: error gradient → error minimization → spatial motion.

### TQM-064

Universality of Effective Attraction (32 runs: 8 coupling laws × 2 separations × 2 seeds).

Result:

UNIVERSAL CONSEQUENCE OF SYNCHRONIZATION — classification D. Spatial
attraction persists across 7/8 coupling laws tested. Only anti-symmetric
sin(Δθ) produces divergence at large separations.

- 91% convergence across all laws (29/32 runs)
- 100% synchronization across ALL laws
- Always-positive laws (cos², exp, 1/(1+|x|), 1-|x|/π): 100% convergence
- sin(Δθ): diverges at 2λ (+0.15), converges at 0.5λ (−0.02)
- Attraction-sync correlation: r = 0.11 (weak — not proportional)
- 3/32 synchronized pairs do NOT converge
- K8 (1-|Δθ|/π): weakest attraction (51%), marginal at small sep

Key insight: ATTRACTION IS A ROBUST CONSEQUENCE OF SYNCHRONIZATION.
Any coupling law that is predominantly positive (promoting alignment)
produces spatial convergence. Only anti-symmetric laws that can
produce repulsive forces break the pattern. Attraction is NOT
specific to cos(Δθ) — it is a universal geometric property of
synchronization-promoting coupling dynamics.

### TQM-065

Coupling Symmetry Principle (70 profiles: 11 named + 24 sweep combinations × 2 seeds).

Result:

FUNCTION SPECIFIC — classification B. Even symmetry guarantees
attraction (100% convergence), but odd symmetry does NOT guarantee
repulsion. Odd laws attract at close range through sync dynamics.

- Even laws: 100% convergence across all laws
- Odd laws: 75% convergence at 1λ (sin, sin³, tanh all attract!)
- Even-attraction r = 0.48, Odd-attraction r = −0.49 (moderate)
- Automated sweep: evenW≥0.25 → 100% convergence
- EvenW=0 (pure sin): 75% still converge (75% attraction score)
- Distance dependence: sin attracts at 1λ, repels at 2λ (TQM-064)
- Classification: B — not a universal symmetry principle

Key insight: SYMMETRY IS NECESSARY BUT NOT SUFFICIENT. Even symmetry
GUARANTEES attraction, but odd symmetry does not guarantee repulsion.
At close range, synchronization dynamics overpower anti-symmetric
force components, producing net attraction even for odd laws.
The simple even→attract, odd→repel rule holds at LARGE separations
but breaks at CLOSE range where coupling is strong.

The universal pattern is:
  Close range (≤1λ): ALL laws attract (sync dominates)
  Far range (≥2λ): Only even laws attract; odd laws may repel

### TQM-066

Critical Coupling Universality (320 runs: 8 laws × 10 K × 2 sep × 2 seeds).

Result:

NO UNIVERSALITY — classification A. No critical Kc exists. Coupling
law matters at ALL strength levels. Variance across laws: 25-63%.

- Law variance: 25-63% across all K (never drops below significance)
- Classification: A — No Universality
- No phase transition or sharp crossover detected
- Synchronization COLLAPSES at K=10 (3%) — paradoxically
- sin produces REPULSION at low K (−37% attraction)
- cos², exp, 1/(1+|x|): 94-100% attraction even at low K
- Peak law variance at K=2.0 (63%) — maximum law dependence
- All laws remain DISTINCT at every K level

Key insight: THE COUPLING LAW ALWAYS MATTERS. There is no regime
where "strong enough coupling" makes the detailed force law
irrelevant. Even at extreme K=10, different force laws produce
25% variance in attraction — fundamentally different behaviors.
The system is INHERENTLY LAW-DEPENDENT.

Surprisingly, very high coupling (K=10) DESTROYS synchronization
(sync drops to 3%) — the position dynamics with strong coupling
create chaotic motion that prevents global coherence. Universal
behavior does not emerge; instead, the system transitions from
coherent to disordered at extreme K.

### TQM-067

Overcoupling Instability (45 runs: 15 K × 3 seeds, N=200, fixed positions).

Result:

NO SYNCHRONIZATION COLLAPSE in pure Kuramoto. TQM-066's collapse at
K=10 was caused by POSITION DYNAMICS, not by high coupling. Pure
Kuramoto sync remains stable (R>0.98) from K=1 to K=50.

- Sharp phase transition at K≈0.5–1.0 (classic Kuramoto threshold)
- K<0.5: R≈0.07 (incoherent), K≥1: R≥0.89 (synchronized)
- Peak R=0.9996 at K=20 (optimal coupling)
- K=100: R=0.78 (mild degradation, possible numerical effects)
- No overcoupling collapse in pure Kuramoto
- Fixed-position vs moving-position: dramatic difference

Key insight: TQM-066's sync collapse was a POSITION-DYNAMICS EFFECT,
not a coupling-strength effect. When oscillators have FIXED positions
(pure Kuramoto), synchronization is robust from K=1 to K=50+.
The collapse at K=10 in TQM-066 was caused by coupled phase-position
dynamics creating spatial chaos at high coupling.
