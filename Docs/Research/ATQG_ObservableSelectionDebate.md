# AT-QG Observable Selection Debate

**Status:** REFEREE DEBATE NOTE — SELECTION RISK REMAINS

---

## Objection

The strongest remaining objection is that AT still looks **catalog-driven at the observable level**. The audited register contains only a **minor natural core** (`7/29`) while the majority of observables are classified as **secondary** (`19/29`), with a nontrivial **post-hoc** minority (`3/29`). That means the theory’s strongest structural claims do not yet translate into a globally unique observable target set. In particular, some of the most visible cosmology and coupling items are either secondary readouts or explicitly post-hoc sensitive, so the concern is not that the framework fails, but that it may explain the right class of observable after the target has already been effectively chosen.

## Defense

The strongest referee-safe defense is that AT does **not** claim uniform prediction strength across the full register. It distinguishes:
- a **natural core** of pre-registered or hidden-target observables,
- a large body of **secondary** observables that are structurally constrained but catalog-matched,
- and a small **post-hoc** minority that is explicitly flagged.

On that reading, AT’s defensible core is not “everything is uniquely predicted,” but rather: the D96 spectrum, operator basis, and moment hierarchy are structurally forced; a small set of observables were fixed before measurement or hidden from measurement; and the remainder are calibrated reconstructions of known targets. This is consistent with the documented audits and with the natural predictive core.

## Residual Risk

Medium. The risk is not that the framework lacks internal structure, but that the observable map is not globally unique and still leans on catalog-informed target selection for most of the register.

## Publication Wording

“AT reconstructs a structurally forced core of observables, while a larger secondary register remains catalog-informed and a small post-hoc minority is explicitly flagged. The theory’s strongest claims are therefore structural and calibration-based rather than globally unique at the level of observable target selection.”
