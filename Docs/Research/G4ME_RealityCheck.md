## 2. Results

### (a) The flat curve is the marginal α=0 member of the self-similar family (G4-ME40)

The general self-similar deficit family m(r) ∝ r^(−α) (α≠0), with the log deficit m ∝ ln(Rmax/r) at α=0,
gives a rotation-curve ratio v²(3)/v²(9) = 3^α:

| α | v²(3)/v²(9) | shape |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (log) | 1.18 | flat |
| 0.25 | 1.49 | mild falloff |
| 0.5 | 1.90 | mild falloff |
| 1.0 | 3.15 | Keplerian |
| 2.0 | 9.09 | steep falloff |

The flat curve is EXACTLY the α=0 (log) case — the boundary between falling (α>0) and rising (α<0)
curves.

### (b) The log deficit is the unique scale-invariant profile (G4-ME41)

The per-octave increment m(r) − m(λr) is the signature of self-similarity. For the log deficit it is
CONSTANT (= m₀·ln2/ln(Rmax/r₀) = 0.0926, spread < 1e-6), while for a power law (α=1) it DECAYS (0.105 →
0.018, a 6× drop). A constant per-octave increment means the profile is scale-invariant: rescaling just
shifts it by a constant. This is precisely why its gradient a ∝ 1/r is the unique scale-free radial field.

### (c) Constant-per-octave growth yields the log deficit (G4-ME42)

A self-similar growth process — each octave adds an EQUAL increment m₀/K — accumulates to the discrete
staircase m(r) = m₀(K−k)/K ∝ ln(Rmax/r). The cumulative equal increments reproduce the discrete hierarchy
exactly, and match the log-deficit envelope to ≤14% in the inner octaves.

## 3. Classification: SEMI-NATURAL

The log-deficit hierarchy is **not a tuned ansatz**, but it is a **special (marginal) self-similar case**:

- **Natural:** the log profile is the UNIQUE scale-invariant deficit (constant per-octave increment),
  generated by the natural constant-per-octave growth of actualization deficits — the expected profile of
  a scale-free process (the temporal field has no preferred scale).
- **Marginal:** it is the α=0 boundary of the self-similar power-law family m ∝ r^(−α); any α≠0 (also
  self-similar) gives a non-flat curve, and the dynamic selection of α=0 is a symmetry assumption rather
  than a derived attractor.
- **Free parameters:** m₀ (total deficit) and Rmax (galaxy radius) remain free — but these correspond to
  total mass and size, which are also free in GR.

---

## 4. Conclusion

The flat rotation curve is the self-similar (scale-free) prediction of the TQM abundance law, not a
fine-tuned ansatz: the log deficit m ∝ ln(Rmax/r) is the unique profile with constant per-octave
increment, so it is what a scale-invariant actualization process generates by default. It is, however, the
marginal α=0 member of the family — a natural-but-special selection whose dynamical origin (why α=0 is
chosen over other self-similar α) remains an open question.
