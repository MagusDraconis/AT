## Purpose

NP_115 completed the epistemic chain up to understanding. NP_116 asks the final semantic question:
**what is meaning, and how does it differ from information, knowledge, and understanding?** Program:
(1) define the six terms plus meaning; (2) compare observer / knower / understander; (3) determine
whether meaning is interpreted information / contextual understanding / value-weighted prediction /
relation between models; (4) remove context; (5) remove prediction; (6) determine what survives; (7)
identify the minimum condition for meaning. **Success criterion:** the ontology of meaning,
distinguished from information, knowledge, and understanding. No new primitives; canonical AT
unchanged.

---

## 1. Define the six terms + meaning

| Term | In AT |
|---|---|
| **data** | a raw distinction (D_039) |
| **information** | the distinction structure (M_004) |
| **observation** | a distinction incorporated (NP_113) |
| **knowledge** | predictive integrated distinctions (NP_114) |
| **understanding** | the model of models (NP_115) |
| **meaning** | the SIGNIFICANCE of a distinction — how much it matters for the observer's future actualizations |

**Meaning is the significance dimension** — not "what is this" (information) or "what happens next"
(knowledge/understanding), but "what does this matter for me."

---

## 2. Observer / knower / understander

| Object | What it does |
|---|---|
| **observer** | reads distinctions (NP_111) |
| **knower** | anticipates from the distinctions (NP_114) |
| **understander** | models its own predictions (NP_115) |
| **meaning-bearer** | WEIGHTS the distinctions by their significance (what they change for it) |

Meaning is the step beyond understanding: the observer not only *knows why* but *cares* — it
weights each distinction by the difference it makes to its own future.

---

## 3. A / B / C / D — what is meaning?

| Interpretation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A) interpreted information** | **NECESSARY, not sufficient.** Meaning is interpreted, but interpretation alone (without significance) is just understanding. |
| **B) contextual understanding** | **NECESSARY, not sufficient.** Meaning is contextual, but context alone (without value) is just understanding-in-context. |
| **C) value-weighted prediction** | **YES — the differentiator.** Meaning is the distinction weighted by its CONSEQUENCES for the observer. |
| **D) relation between models** | **YES — the same object.** Meaning is what the world's distinctions MEAN FOR the observer's model — the relation. |

**Determination: C = D (value-weighted prediction = relation between models); A and B are necessary
but not sufficient.** The differentiator is *significance* (value/consequence), which information,
knowledge, and understanding all lack.

---

## 4. Remove context

**Meaning collapses.** Significance is context-dependent: a distinction means something only in its
context. Without context there is no weighting — the distinction is "bare", informative but
meaningless. (Information, knowledge, and understanding survive; meaning does not.)

---

## 5. Remove prediction

**Meaning collapses.** A distinction with no future consequence (nothing it changes for the
observer) is insignificant — it is information, not meaning. Significance IS future consequence, so
meaning requires prediction.

---

## 6. What survives?

| Removed | Survives | Gone |
|---|---|---|
| context | information, knowledge, understanding | **meaning** (no weighting) |
| prediction | information (raw) | **meaning**, knowledge, understanding |

**Meaning is the most fragile and the most dependent** — it needs both context (to weight) and
prediction (to have consequences). Information is the most robust; meaning is the most contingent.

---

## 7. The minimum condition for meaning

**SIGNIFICANCE: the distinction must matter for the observer's future actualizations.** A
distinction has meaning exactly when it is *weighted* — when it changes what the observer will do
next, in a way that matters (has value). This is the "for" of meaning: meaning is always meaning
*for* an observer.

---

> observation → knowledge → understanding → meaning, each adding a dimension (read → structure →
> incorporate → predict → model → WEIGHT). Meaning is DISTINCT from information (the raw structure),
> knowledge (the prediction), and understanding (the model of models): its differentiator is

## 8. Counterexamples

| Attempt | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "meaning = information" | information is the raw structure; meaning is its significance (value) |
| "meaning = understanding" | understanding models the predictions; meaning weights them by consequence |
| "meaning needs a new primitive" | meaning is significance — a relation of the already-derived structures |
| "meaning survives without context" | significance is context-dependent; a bare distinction is meaningless |
| "meaning survives without prediction" | no future consequence = insignificant = no meaning |

---

## 9. Falsification paths

| Claim | Falsification |
|---|---|
| meaning = value-weighted prediction | a meaning that carries no consequence for the observer's future |
| the minimum condition is significance | a distinction that matters (has meaning) without weighting the observer's future |
| meaning ≠ information/knowledge/understanding | a meaning identical to the raw structure, the prediction, or the model |

**Conclusion.** Meaning is **value-weighted prediction — the significance of a distinction for an
observer**: how much the distinction matters for the observer's future actualizations. It is the
apex of the epistemic chain (data → information → observation → knowledge → understanding →
meaning), distinct from all four below it by its *significance* dimension. It requires both context
(to weight) and prediction (to have consequences), and its minimum condition is that the
distinction must matter for the observer. No new primitive; canonical AT unchanged.
