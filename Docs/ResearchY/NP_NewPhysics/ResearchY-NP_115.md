## Purpose

NP_114 established knowledge = predictive integrated distinctions. NP_115 asks the final epistemic
question: **what is understanding, and how does it differ from knowledge?** Program: (1) define
data / information / observation / memory / knowledge / understanding; (2) compare detector /
recorder / predictor / understander; (3) determine whether understanding is more knowledge /
compression / predictive hierarchy / model of models; (4) remove prediction; (5) remove
integration; (6) find the minimum condition separating knowledge from understanding; (7) test
whether understanding changes future actualization more effectively. **Success criterion:** the
ontology of understanding, distinguished from knowledge. No new primitives; canonical AT unchanged.

| **knowledge** | predictive integrated distinctions (NP_114) |
| **understanding** | the MODEL OF MODELS — knowledge compressed and made self-referential ("why", not just "what next") |

---

## 2. Detector / recorder / predictor / understander

| Object | What it does |
|---|---|
| **detector** | reads ONE distinction (NP_111) |
| **recorder** | stores the distinctions (memory) |
| **predictor** | anticipates from the integrated distinctions (knowledge, NP_114) |
| **understander** | holds a compressed, self-referential model of its own predictions (the "why") |

The chain is **cumulative**: each stage adds a capacity — read → store → predict → *model the
prediction itself*.

---

## 3. A / B / C / D — what is understanding?

| Interpretation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A) more knowledge** | **REFUTED.** Understanding is a qualitatively higher order, not a larger pile of knowledge. |
| **B) compression of knowledge** | **YES.** Understanding is knowledge compressed to its minimal predictive model (economy, the I_occ-style compression). |
| **C) predictive hierarchy** | **YES.** Understanding is predictions *about* predictions — a hierarchy of anticipations. |
| **D) model of models** | **YES — the answer.** Understanding is self-referential: it knows *why* it predicts, so it can re-derive, transfer, and generalize. |

**Determination: B = C = D (compression of knowledge = predictive hierarchy = model of models);
A (more knowledge) is refuted.**

---

## 4. Remove prediction

**Both knowledge and understanding collapse.** Prediction is the common core: knowledge predicts,
understanding predicts *about* predictions. Without prediction there is no anticipation at all —
only observations and memory (a recorder, not a knower or understander).

---

## 5. Remove integration

**Knowledge collapses to scattered observations; understanding collapses to scattered knowledge.**
Integration is what turns observations into knowledge; the *higher-order* integration (the model
of the model) is what turns knowledge into understanding.

---

## 6. The minimum condition separating knowledge from understanding

**COMPRESSION + SELF-REFERENCE.** Knowledge predicts; understanding knows *why* it predicts — it
compresses the knowledge into a minimal model that re-derives the predictions and *transfers* to
novel cases. The separator is the step from "what happens next" to "what makes it happen" — the
self-referential model.

```
knowledge      = predictive distinctions          ("it will rain")
understanding  = the compressed model of those     ("clouds bring rain, so this sky → rain")
                 predictions (self-referential)
```

---

## 7. Does understanding change future actualization more effectively?

**YES.** Understanding is the *compressed* model — it generalizes to novel cases (transfer) and
re-derives from fewer distinctions (economy), so it shapes future actualization across a **wider**
range than rote knowledge. It is the efficiency of prediction: the compressed model anticipates
correctly in situations knowledge (mere stored cases) has never seen.
