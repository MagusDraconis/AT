## Purpose

NP_106 established that Difference must actualize; its remaining boundary was labeled "one outcome
per tick". NP_107 asks the follow-up: **why does actualization occur as discrete ticks rather than
continuously?** Program: (1) remove the tick; (2) replace with continuous actualization; (3) test
whether counting / Born selection / localization / persistence survive; (4) determine whether
discreteness is logical necessity / emergent / framework choice / irreducible boundary; (5) find the
earliest place continuity fails; (6) compare discrete vs continuous Difference; (7) determine whether
actualization without ticks is coherent. **Success criterion:** explain why reality actualizes
tick-by-tick. No new primitives; canonical AT unchanged.

---

## 1. Remove the tick — continuous actualization

```
replace discrete ticks with CONTINUOUS actualization:
   → counting: FAILS (a continuum has no countable normalization Σρ = 1)
   → Born selection: FAILS ("one outcome per event" is discrete; a continuum has no single outcome)
   → localization: FAILS (a wave packet needs DISCRETE nodes; a continuum has no positions)
   → persistence: FAILS (persistence is "across ticks"; no ticks → no persistence)
```

**Continuous actualization breaks everything.** The discrete tick is not an optional refinement —
it is what makes counting, selection, localization, and persistence possible.

---

## 2. Why discreteness — the binary nature of Difference

```
Difference  =  "this ≠ that"  =  a BINARY relation  =  a discrete CUT
```

A Difference is **binary** — two terms, sharply separated (NP_079: scale-freeness was already
derived from this binary nature). A binary relation is **discrete**: it draws a *cut*, not a
*gradient*. Therefore the act of drawing the distinction (actualization, NP_106) is **discrete** —
one outcome (one side of the binary) per act. The tick's discreteness is the discreteness of the
cut itself.

---

**Continuity fails at the very first step — the binary nature of Difference.** A Difference is a
cut, not a continuum; every structure built on it (counting, selection, the phase lattice) inherits
that discreteness.

## 4. Discrete vs continuous Difference

| | Discrete Difference (binary) | Continuous Difference |
|---|---|---|
| form | a sharp CUT ("this ≠ that") | a gradient (A blends into B) |
| coherent? | YES — the distinction is drawn | **NO** — the distinction is blurred into a continuum |

**A "continuous Difference" is a contradiction**: a continuum blurs A and B together, so there is no
distinction — undifferentiated unity, refuted by NP_104. Difference can only be discrete.

## 5. A / B / C / D — what is discreteness?

| Determination | Verdict |

| **B) emergent** | **NO.** Discreteness is not an emergent higher-level feature; it is built into the binary nature. |

**Determination: A (logical necessity).** This REFINES QG011: the "deepest boundary" is not the
tick's discreteness but Difference's *binary nature* itself. The tick's discreteness is DERIVED.

## 6. Is actualization without ticks coherent?

**NO.** Continuous actualization blurs the binary cut into a continuum, destroying the distinction
(undifferentiated unity, refuted by NP_104). Discrete ticks are the only coherent form of
actualization, because they are the only form that preserves the binary nature of Difference.
