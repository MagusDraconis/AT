## Purpose

NP_109 ranked condensed matter as MISSING (never mapped). NP_110 asks the gateway question: **can
the Difference → D96 ontology naturally generate collective matter, or does condensed matter lie
beyond D96?** Program: (1) inventory collective systems (crystal, lattice, phonon, magnet,
superconductor); (2) determine whether they are resonance hierarchies / phase-locked networks /
deficit clusters / new ontology; (3) construct single → bound pair → repeated bound network; (4)
test emergence of collective modes, phonons, band structure; (5) locate the first failure point; (6)
determine whether condensed matter needs a new primitive / geometry / symmetry / or only larger
networks. **Success criterion:** determine whether condensed matter lies beyond D96 or is the next
natural layer above binding and hierarchy. No new primitives; canonical AT unchanged.

## 5. The first failure point

**The cubic O_h anisotropy (the SAME failure as nuclear structure, NP_089).** The lattice dispersion
is isotropic to leading order (ω ≈ c·k) but the cubic correction −(k_x⁴+k_y⁴+k_z⁴)/12 breaks O(3)
down to O_h at finite k. Therefore:

| Collective property | Exact in D96? |
|---|---|
| linear acoustic phonons (long wavelength) | **YES to leading order** (isotropic) |
| exact isotropic phonon dispersion | **NO** — the cubic correction survives at finite k |
| exact band-structure degeneracies | **NO** — split by the cubic anisotropy (as the 2l+1 multiplets were) |

**The first failure point is the same O(3)-approximate weakness:** condensed matter's qualitative
ontology (crystal = repeated bound network, phonons = collective modes) is DERIVED, but its exact
quantitative properties (isotropic dispersion, band degeneracies) inherit the cubic anisotropy.
