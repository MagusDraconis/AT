## 3. First unresolved node

**The internal-3 upper bound / count saturation.**

Formally it is the conjunction of two faces:

| Face | Residual | No-go | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — multiplicity | $N\le3$ (upper bound) | **T-10** | 0.70 |
| B — gauge count | $n=3$ (defect count) | **T-09** | 0.10 |

Both are underived; both point at the same integer 3; neither theorem implies the
other, and no linking mechanism (A-10) connects them. The node is therefore **one
problem with two faces**, not two problems.

## 4. Strongest candidate mechanism

**m=3 closure** ($\Omega=(q+3)/q$, $\gamma=1/\Omega$, rational mode-locking) is the
strongest *structural* candidate: it is the only proposed mechanism that targets the
node directly and is self-contained. Its status from the accepted audits:

- **New mathematics** (not equivalent/integrated/contradicted — m=3 reconciliation).
- **Targets exactly** the Phase-151 gap (no principle bounds $N\le3$).
- **PATH, not a theorem** — the closure-order derivation lives in the external TRM repo
  ("strongly constrained path", not theorem-level).
- **UNMAPPED** — $\Omega\approx1.16$–$1.19$, $\gamma\approx0.84$–$0.86$ have no assigned
  observable; no $m=3\leftrightarrow N$ or $m=3\leftrightarrow n$ map exists; $\gamma\ne2/3$
  (no Koide link).

**Runner-up (repo-internal, quantitative):** promote the **Higgs-vacuum-stability bound**
(heavy 4th generation ⇒ $\lambda<0$ below Planck) to a categorical $N\le3$ theorem
(Phase 151). Weakness: model-dependent and quantitative, not categorical.

*Ranking:* m=3 closure (structural, targets the node) > Higgs-stability (quantitative,
model-dependent). Both are currently non-closing.

## 5. Strongest no-go theorem

**T-10 ($N\le3$ no-go, confidence 0.70)** — no stability/anomaly/representation/defect/
information principle bounds $N\le3$; $N\ge4$ is CONTINGENT.

This is the firmest barrier: it is the *reason* the node exists, and it is the wall the
candidate mechanism (m=3) must overcome. Note the asymmetry with the gauge face:

| No-go | Target | Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-10** | $N\le3$ | **0.70** | firmly closed — the multiplicity face is hard to derive |
| T-09 | $n=3$ | 0.10 | weakly closed — the *count* face is actually **more open** (a principle may still exist) |

The asymmetry implies: **the most promising entry point to the node is the gauge-count
face (n=3), not the family-count face (N≤3)** — precisely because T-09 is the weaker
no-go. (No claim is made here about whether m=3 maps to $n$; the audits record only that
it is unmapped to both.)

## 6. Conclusion

The Internal-3 Problem is **one node** — the saturation of internal multiplicity/count at
3 — with **two faces** ($N\le3$ multiplicity, $n=3$ gauge count), **no linking mechanism**
(A-10), **one candidate mechanism** (m=3 closure, a path not a theorem), and **one
dominant no-go** (T-10 at 0.70, with the weaker T-09 at 0.10 marking the gauge face as
the more tractable entry point).

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| First unresolved node | internal-3 upper bound / count saturation |
| Strongest candidate mechanism | m=3 closure (path, unmapped) |
| Strongest no-go theorem | T-10 ($N\le3$, 0.70) |
| Same / Related / Independent | 1 Same · 5 Related · 0 Independent |

No new physics is invented; no new primitives introduced. This is a synthesis of Phases
149–151, the Why-3 Meta Audit, and the m=3 closure reconciliation audits.
