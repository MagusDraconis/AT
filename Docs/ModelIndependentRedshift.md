# Model-Independent Redshift (QG-081)

Does redshift alone require expanding space? **No.**

Redshift is a frequency ratio `1+z = ν_emit/ν_obs`. It is a direct observation of
spectral lines; it carries no metric by itself. At least three mechanisms can produce it:

1. **Metric expansion** (ΛCDM): wavelength stretches with `a(t)`, `1+z = a0/a`.
2. **Gravitational redshift**: `1+z = √(g00_obs/g00_emit)` — negligible at cosmological scales.
3. **Evolving clock rate** (Time-Scale Cosmology, QG-080): `1+z = γ_obs/γ_emit` with `γ = dτ/dt`.

## What discriminates the interpretations

Redshift alone cannot distinguish these. The discriminator is **time dilation**:

- ΛCDM and weak TSC (γ=a) predict light-curve stretch `∝ (1+z)^1` (b = +1).
- A "strong" static-space model with clocks faster in the past predicts `∝ (1+z)^−1` (b = −1).

The measured SN Ia value `b = 1.00 ± 0.05` excludes the strong static model at ~40σ and
selects either expanding space **or** the conformal-time (γ=a) clock — which are the same
thing. So redshift + time dilation together do not uniquely demand expanding space; they
demand a specific `(1+z)` clock/dilation structure that conformal FLRW satisfies.

## Model-independent statements
- "Light from distant sources is redshifted by `1+z`" — observed.
- "Distant processes run slower by `(1+z)`" — observed (SN Ia, quasar variability).
- "Space physically expands" — an interpretation, not an observation.
