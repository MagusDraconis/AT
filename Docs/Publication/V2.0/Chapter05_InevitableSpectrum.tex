\chapter{The Inevitable Spectrum}
\label{c05:chap:spectrum}

\section{Introduction}
\label{c05:sec:spectrum-introduction}

The canonical hierarchy of The Actualization Theory
\begin{equation}
\label{c05:eq:core}
\text{Difference}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Actualization}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Inevitable Spectrum}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Physics}
\end{equation}
is complete as a derivation structure (Chapter~\ref{c04:chap:closure-minimal},
Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:minimal-hierarchy}). This chapter develops the third member of the
hierarchy: the \emph{inevitable spectrum}. We establish that the spectrum is not a primitive
of the theory but the \emph{derived output} of the actualization process: the dynamics
converge to a unique, content-independent attractor of size $N=96$; the attractor is a
stable fixed point, not a freely chosen input; and the spectrum is the eigenspectrum of that
attractor, carrying no independent choice \cite{c05:QG116,c05:QG282,c05:QG295}.

We then establish the \emph{uniqueness} and \emph{necessity} of the spectrum: the
attractor is unique \cite{c05:QG116}, the size $N=96$ is inevitable --- not a choice
\cite{c05:QG159,c05:QG160} --- and the spectrum is a deterministic function of the converged
network. This completes the canonical core Difference → Actualization → Spectrum: the first
three members of the hierarchy are now established as primitive, derived, and inevitable
output respectively. The consequences for physics --- that all observable sectors are reads
of this one spectrum --- close the chapter and open the physics chapters that follow
\cite{c05:QG293,c05:QG294,c05:QG296}.

The results of this chapter are the canonical end-state of the spectrum-necessity program
\cite{c05:QG295,c05:QG296}. Throughout we use only accepted canonical results and introduce no new
physics and no speculation.

\section{The Actualization Attractor}
\label{c05:sec:attractor}

\begin{definition}[Actualization attractor]
\label{c05:def:attractor}
The \emph{actualization attractor} is the converged state of the actualization process: the
configuration to which every initial pattern of Difference's count-producing dynamics
converges, with zero residual change.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The dynamics converge]
\label{c05:thm:converge}
The actualization dynamics converge: the residual link growth reaches zero, and the
topology saturates at a stable final state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The actualization-structures program \cite{c05:QG116} establishes that the actualization
dynamics converges with zero residual link growth: the self-reinforcing feedback (activity
to links to activity) is bounded by network capacity, so the topology saturates. This
convergence is the same saturation established by the Closure Principle
(Chapter~\ref{c04:chap:closure-minimal}, Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-holds}): the boundary of the
process is its stable fixed point.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The attractor is unique]
\label{c05:thm:attractor-unique}
The actualization attractor is unique and content-independent: every initial activity
pattern converges to the same final geometry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The actualization-structures program \cite{c05:QG116} establishes the content-independence of
the attractor: identical link counts and identical span are reached from every initial
pattern. Combined with the convergence of Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:converge}, this shows the
attractor is unique --- there is one final geometry, not a family --- and independent of the
starting configuration.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The attractor is the closure]
\label{c05:cor:attractor-closure}
The unique actualization attractor is the closure fixed point of the theory: the same
stable state at which the process closes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:converge} and Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique}, the attractor is the
unique converged state of the dynamics. By the Closure Principle
(Chapter~\ref{c04:chap:closure-minimal}, Definition~\ref{c04:def:closure-principle}), the boundary
of the theory is the fixed point of the process. The unique attractor and the closure fixed
point are the same configuration.
\end{proof}

\section{The Fixed Point \texorpdfstring{$N=96$}{N=96}}
\label{c05:sec:n96}

\begin{definition}[The \texorpdfstring{$N=96$}{N=96} attractor]
\label{c05:def:n96}
The \emph{$N=96$ attractor} is the unique converged network of the actualization process:
the stable fixed point whose size is $N=96$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The attractor is \texorpdfstring{$N=96$}{N=96}, not a choice]
\label{c05:thm:n96-attractor}
The size $N=96$ of the converged network is the attractor of the dynamics, not a freely
chosen input: it is the inevitable fixed point of the actualization process.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum-necessity audit \cite{c05:QG295} establishes that $N=96$ is the attractor, not a
choice: the actualization flow has a stable fixed point at $N=96$, and the boundary is the
closure of that flow (the Closure Principle \cite{c05:QG282}). The inevitability is further
supported by the D96-selection program: the size $96$ is selected as the unique complete-Z2
natural size with the three-family octave window \cite{c05:QG159,c05:QG160}. Hence $N=96$ is an
output of the dynamics --- the unique stable fixed point --- and is not an input or a free
parameter.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No freedom in the size]
\label{c05:cor:n96-no-freedom}
There is no freedom in the size of the network: the actualization process converges to
$N=96$ from every initial pattern, and no other size is a stable fixed point of the
dynamics.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:n96-attractor}, $N=96$ is the unique stable fixed point of the
actualization flow, content-independent (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique}). The
D96-selection program \cite{c05:QG159,c05:QG160} further establishes that no other natural size
satisfies the complete-Z2 and three-family conditions. Hence the size is fixed by the
dynamics and the selection structure; there is no freedom.
\end{proof}

\section{Spectrum Generation}
\label{c05:sec:spectrum-generation}

\begin{definition}[Spectrum generation]
\label{c05:def:spectrum-generation}
\emph{Spectrum generation} is the derivation of the spectrum from the converged network:
the spectrum is the eigenspectrum of the graph Laplacian of the $N=96$ attractor.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The spectrum is the eigenspectrum of the attractor]
\label{c05:thm:spectrum-eigenspectrum}
The spectrum of the theory is the Laplacian eigenspectrum of the converged $N=96$ network:
the $95$ positive modes with a single zero mode (the background), a deterministic function
of the attractor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum-necessity audit \cite{c05:QG295} establishes that the spectrum is the Laplacian
eigenspectrum of the converged network: the graph Laplacian of the $N=96$ attractor has
$95$ positive eigenvalues (the modes) and one zero eigenvalue (the background). Since the
attractor is unique and deterministic (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique},
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:n96-attractor}), the spectrum is a deterministic function of it --- an
output of the dynamics, not an additional input.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The spectral constants]
\label{c05:cor:spectral-constants}
The spectral constants of the theory --- the moments $\Sigma m=95$,
$\Sigma\sqrt{m}=64.08$, $\Sigma m^2=229$, the occupancy moment $1900.25$, the span $6.40$,
and the doublet multiplicities $[42\times2,5,6]$ --- are outputs of the spectrum, hence
outputs of the attractor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The spectral constants are moments and structural features of the spectrum. By
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-eigenspectrum}, the spectrum is the deterministic eigenspectrum of
the attractor. Hence the constants are functions of the attractor --- derived quantities,
not primitive inputs. This is the canonical reading of the spectrum layer of the minimal
hierarchy \cite{c05:QG294}.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniqueness of the Spectrum}
\label{c05:sec:spectrum-uniqueness}

\begin{theorem}[The spectrum is unique]
\label{c05:thm:spectrum-unique}
The spectrum of the theory is unique: there is exactly one spectrum, the eigenspectrum of
the unique actualization attractor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The attractor is unique (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique}) and its size is fixed at
$N=96$ (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:n96-attractor}). The spectrum is the eigenspectrum of that
attractor (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-eigenspectrum}), and the eigenspectrum of a given
network is unique. Hence the spectrum is unique: one attractor, one eigenspectrum, one
spectrum of the theory.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No spectrum choice]
\label{c05:cor:no-spectrum-choice}
The spectrum carries no choice: it is the unique output of the unique attractor, and no
spectral feature is freely adjustable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-unique}, the spectrum is unique; by
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:spectral-constants}, its constants are outputs of the attractor; by
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:n96-no-freedom}, the attractor carries no freedom. Hence the spectrum
carries no choice at any level.
\end{proof}

\section{Spectrum Necessity}
\label{c05:sec:spectrum-necessity}

\begin{theorem}[The spectrum is not primitive]
\label{c05:thm:spectrum-not-primitive}
The spectrum is not a primitive of the theory: it is the inevitable output of the
actualization attractor, carrying no choice and presupposing no independent input.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum-necessity audit \cite{c05:QG295} poses exactly this question --- is the spectrum
fundamental, or the inevitable fixed point of actualization? --- and establishes the latter.
The spectrum is the eigenspectrum of the converged network (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-eigenspectrum}),
which is the unique, fixed-size, content-independent attractor
(Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique}, Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:n96-attractor}). It therefore
presupposes the actualization process and its primitive Difference, and is an output, not an
input. The spectrum is inevitable: it is not a primitive.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The spectrum is necessary for physics]
\label{c05:thm:spectrum-necessary}
The spectrum is a necessary member of the hierarchy: physics is the readout of the spectrum,
so without the spectrum there is no physics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reconstruction audit \cite{c05:QG296} establishes that all major results of the theory are
reconstructed from the minimal hierarchy including the spectrum layer. Physics is the
readout of the spectrum (Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization},
Corollary~\ref{c03:cor:physics-readout}): the sectors are projection classes over the operator
layer of the one spectrum. Hence the spectrum is necessary: it is the indispensable bridge
between the actualization process and the observable physics.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Inevitable and necessary]
\label{c05:cor:inevitable-necessary}
The spectrum is both inevitable (an output, not a primitive) and necessary (required for
physics): it is the forced output of the process that physics requires.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Inevitability: Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-not-primitive}. Necessity:
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-necessary}. The two properties are compatible: the spectrum is
forced by the dynamics (inevitable) and indispensable to the derivation (necessary) --- it
is the inevitable output of the actualization attractor from which all physics is read.
\end{proof}

The first three members of the canonical hierarchy are now established: Difference is the
primitive (Chapter~\ref{c01:chap:difference}), Actualization is the derived process face of
Difference (Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}), and the Spectrum is the inevitable output of
the actualization attractor (this chapter). The canonical core

\label{c05:eq:core-complete}
\text{Difference}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Actualization}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Inevitable Spectrum}

\begin{proof}
The three members are established as: primitive (Difference,

(Actualization, Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:derived-not-primitive} of
Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}), and inevitable output (Spectrum,
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-not-primitive}). The completion of the canonical core is the
completion of the minimal hierarchy's first three members \cite{c05:QG293,c05:QG294}; the fourth
member, Physics, is the readout of the spectrum, developed in the physics chapters that
follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The minimal hierarchy is fully anchored]
\label{c05:cor:hierarchy-anchored}
The minimal hierarchy Difference → Actualization → Spectrum → Physics is now fully anchored:
its first three members are established, and Physics is the spectrum readout developed in
the subsequent chapters.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first three members are established by Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:core-completed}; the fourth is
the readout of the spectrum (Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization},
Corollary~\ref{c03:cor:physics-readout}). Hence every member of the minimal hierarchy has a
canonical status: primitive, derived process, inevitable output, emergent readout.

\section{Consequences for Physics}
\label{c05:sec:physics-consequences}

\begin{lemma}[Physics is the readout of the inevitable spectrum]
\label{c05:lem:physics-readout}
All observable physics is the readout of the inevitable spectrum: the resonance structure
projected by the operator layer, from which the sectors of physics emerge.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum is the unique, inevitable output of the attractor
(Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-unique}, Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-not-primitive}). The sectors
of physics are projection classes over the operator layer of the one spectrum
(Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}, Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:resonance-readout}). Hence physics is
the readout of the spectrum: every sector --- masses, couplings, mixings, gravity,
cosmology --- is a projection of the one actualization-driven spectrum
\cite{c05:QG272}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No physics without the spectrum]
\label{c05:thm:no-physics-without-spectrum}
There is no physics in the theory without the spectrum: every derived observable is a
function of the spectral structure, and no observable bypasses the spectrum layer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reconstruction audit \cite{c05:QG296} reconstructs the major results of the theory from the
minimal hierarchy, with the spectrum layer as the source of the spectral constants used by
all sectors. By Lemma~\ref{c05:lem:physics-readout}, physics is the readout of the spectrum.
Hence every observable passes through the spectrum layer; there is no physics without it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The spectrum determines the observable space]
\label{c05:cor:spectrum-determines}
The observable space of the theory is determined by the inevitable spectrum: the set of
derived observables is the set of reads of the unique spectrum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{c05:lem:physics-readout} and Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:no-physics-without-spectrum}, every
observable is a read of the spectrum and none bypasses it. By
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-unique}, the spectrum is unique. Hence the observable space is the
set of reads of the one inevitable spectrum.
\end{proof}

\section{Summary}
\label{c05:sec:spectrum-summary}

This chapter has established the third member of the canonical architecture. We have proved:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The attractor} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:converge},
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:attractor-unique}, Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:attractor-closure}): the
actualization dynamics converge to a unique, content-independent attractor, which is the
closure fixed point;
\item \textbf{The fixed point $N=96$} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:n96-attractor},
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:n96-no-freedom}): the attractor is $N=96$ --- the inevitable stable
fixed point, not a choice;
\item \textbf{Spectrum generation} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-eigenspectrum},
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:spectral-constants}): the spectrum is the Laplacian eigenspectrum of the
converged network, and its constants are derived outputs;
\item \textbf{Uniqueness} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-unique},
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:no-spectrum-choice}): the spectrum is unique and carries no choice;
\item \textbf{Necessity} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-not-primitive},
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:spectrum-necessary}, Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:inevitable-necessary}): the
spectrum is not a primitive but the inevitable output required for physics;
\item \textbf{The core completed} (Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:core-completed},
Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:hierarchy-anchored}): the canonical core
Difference → Actualization → Spectrum is complete;
\item \textbf{Consequences for physics} (Lemma~\ref{c05:lem:physics-readout},
Theorem~\ref{c05:thm:no-physics-without-spectrum}, Corollary~\ref{c05:cor:spectrum-determines}):
physics is the readout of the inevitable spectrum, which determines the observable space.
\end{enumerate}

The inevitable spectrum is therefore the forced output of the actualization process: the
unique eigenspectrum of the $N=96$ attractor, carrying no choice, presupposing no primitive
status, and providing the structure from which all physics is read. The canonical core
Difference → Actualization → Spectrum is complete; the physics chapters that follow derive
each sector as a read of this one spectrum.
