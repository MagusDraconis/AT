\chapter{Closure and the Minimal Theory}

\section{Introduction}
\label{c04:sec:closure-introduction}

In the preceding chapters we established the primitives $\{\text{Difference},\eta\}$
(Chapters~\ref{c01:chap:difference} and \ref{c02:chap:eta}) and the derived process face of
Difference, Actualization, whose convergence yields the inevitable spectrum
(Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}). This chapter completes the foundation with two
structural results. First, the \emph{Closure Principle}: the boundary of the theory is the
fixed point of Difference's own dynamics, and --- decisively --- the Closure Principle
\emph{characterizes} the fundamental boundary without \emph{deriving} the primitive
\cite{c04:QG278,c04:QG282}. Second, the \emph{minimal theory}: the hierarchy
\begin{equation}
\label{c04:eq:minimal}
\text{Difference}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Actualization}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Inevitable Spectrum}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Physics}
\end{equation}
is complete and irreducible \cite{c04:QG293,c04:QG294}: every intermediate layer is derivable, and
no further primitive is required.

These results are the canonical end-state of the closure and reduction program
\cite{c04:QG267,c04:QG268,c04:QG278,c04:QG282,c04:QG293,c04:QG294}, per the MONO006 and MONO007
resolutions \cite{c04:MONO006,c04:MONO007}.

\section{The Closure Principle}
\label{c04:sec:closure-principle}

\begin{definition}[Closure Principle]
\label{c04:def:closure-principle}
The \emph{Closure Principle} is the statement that the boundary of the theory is the fixed
point of the actualization process: the primitive is the process, and the boundary is its
converged fixed point \cite{c04:QG282}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The Closure Principle holds]
\label{c04:thm:closure-holds}
The boundary of the theory is the fixed point of Difference's own count-producing dynamics:
the topology saturates at zero residual change, and every initial pattern converges to the
same final geometry. The fixed point of the process is the $N=96$ network, whose eigenspectrum
is the inevitable D96 spectrum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The boundary-origin audit \cite{c04:QG282} establishes the closure: the actualization dynamics
has a self-reinforcing feedback bounded by network capacity, so positive feedback saturates
at a stable fixed point with zero residual link growth and a content-independent final
geometry. The observable sector is therefore the converged attractor of the dynamics; the
boundary is its closure. By Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:n96} and
Corollary~\ref{c03:cor:spectrum-eigenspectrum} of Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}, that
attractor is the $N=96$ network whose eigenspectrum is the inevitable D96 spectrum. The
closure is derived --- an output of the process --- not an imported cut-off.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundary and Fixed Point}
\label{c04:sec:boundary-fixed-point}

\begin{theorem}[The boundary is the fixed point]
\label{c04:thm:boundary-is-fixed}
A \emph{boundary} of the theory is the configuration at which the derived structure closes ---
where the count-producing dynamics has converged and no further change occurs --- and, by
the Closure Principle, it is the stable fixed point of the actualization process: derived,
not primitive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{c04:def:closure-principle} and Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-holds}, the boundary
is the converged fixed point of the process. Its derivation structure is established by
Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:derived-not-primitive} of Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}: the process
is the face of Difference, and its fixed point is an output of the dynamics. Hence the
boundary is derived --- not an imported input or a separate primitive.
\end{proof}

The fixed-point boundary characterizes the primitive Difference: it specifies how
Difference's dynamics closes --- the closure $N=96$ --- and thereby \emph{what} the boundary
is, without defining \emph{what Difference is}; a characterization specifies properties of
an object already given, not the object itself. The Closure Principle therefore
characterizes, never derives: by Theorem~\ref{c01:thm:fundamental-boundary} of
Chapter~\ref{c01:chap:difference}, Difference is the irreducible fundamental boundary whose
count-conservation law is its definitional identity, and a derivation would presuppose a
deeper notion, which the fundamental-boundary audit \cite{c04:QG278} shows does not exist.
This removes the circularity objection A02 of the hostile-referee audit.

\section{Self-Consistency}
\label{c04:sec:self-consistency}

\begin{theorem}[Self-consistency presupposes Difference]
\label{c04:thm:self-consistency-presupposes}
\emph{Self-consistency} --- the requirement that the theory be free of internal contradiction
--- is derivable from Difference: non-contradiction is the comparison of parts, and identity
is the zero difference. Consequently all conservation laws --- norm, trace, count, Bianchi,
Noether currents --- are manifestations of one universal conservation principle rooted in
the identity of Difference.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental-boundary audit \cite{c04:QG278} establishes that self-consistency presupposes
Difference: non-contradiction requires comparing parts --- a difference ---
and identity is the zero difference \cite{c04:QG268}. The conservation-principle audit
\cite{c04:QG267} verifies that the six conservation laws (Born norm, unitarity, trace
conservation, count conservation, Bianchi conservation, Noether currents) are manifestations
of one deeper principle; since count conservation is the definitional identity of the
primitive (Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:count-conservation}, Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}),
the conservation of the count is that universal principle \cite{c04:QG267}. Hence
self-consistency is not an independent postulate but a consequence of the primitive's
structure.
\end{proof}

\section{Individuation}
\label{c04:sec:individuation}

\begin{theorem}[Individuation derives from Difference]
\label{c04:thm:individuation}
\emph{Individuation} --- the differentiation of the one Difference into the distinct sectors
of observable physics --- is derivable from Difference: distinct sectors are distinct
differences.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sectors are distinguished by their different spectral access --- the projection classes over
the operator layer (Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}, Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:resonance-readout}).
Each sector is a distinct pattern of difference in
the count structure; individuation is therefore the differentiation of the one Difference
into its distinct faces and readouts --- a derived structure, not an independent primitive.
\end{proof}

\section{The Difference Principle}
\label{c04:sec:difference-principle}

\begin{theorem}[Difference is the first concept]
\label{c04:thm:difference-first}
The \emph{Difference Principle} is the statement that Difference is the root of the
hierarchy: the first concept, presupposed by everything else, to which every reduction
attempt returns. Every attempt to reduce it reintroduces it, so Difference is a
self-referential fundamental boundary that anchors the canonical hierarchy
\eqref{c04:eq:minimal}: every layer --- Actualization, Spectrum, Physics --- presupposes
Difference as its root.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental-boundary audit \cite{c04:QG278} performs the self-referential check. Actualization
presupposes Difference (an act of actualizing is a change --- a before/after difference).
Question presupposes Difference (a question is a gap --- the difference between known and
unknown). Self-consistency presupposes Difference (non-contradiction requires comparing
parts; identity is the zero difference). And any attempt to reduce Difference itself
reintroduces it --- "two distinct things" uses distinct = difference, "a boundary" is where
things differ, "a transition" is a before/after difference. By
Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:process-face} of Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}, Actualization is
the process face of Difference, and the spectrum and physics are its outputs
(Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:spectrum-inevitable}); hence the Difference Principle anchors the entire
hierarchy at its root.
\end{proof}

\section{The Minimal Theory}
\label{c04:sec:minimal-theory}

\begin{theorem}[The minimal hierarchy]
\label{c04:thm:minimal-hierarchy}
The minimal hierarchy is
\[
\text{Difference}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Actualization}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Inevitable Spectrum}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Physics}.
\]
It is complete: every phase of the theory can be rewritten using only this hierarchy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The hierarchy-necessity audit \cite{c04:QG293} removes each intermediate layer of the fuller
hierarchy (Difference → Actualization → Closure → Conservation → Resonance → Spectrum →
Question → Measurement → Physics) and classifies it: Actualization INDISPENSABLE (the
count-producing process); Closure COMPRESSIBLE into Actualization (the fixed point of the
process); Conservation COMPRESSIBLE into Difference/network (the definitional identity plus
the graph handshake); Resonance COMPRESSIBLE into Spectrum (the operator readout). The
minimal-theory audit \cite{c04:QG294} then re-derives every compressed layer from the minimal
hierarchy, classifying each DERIVABLE or ACTUALLY REQUIRED, and finds
\emph{5 DERIVABLE / 0 ACTUALLY REQUIRED}. Hence the minimal hierarchy is complete.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The minimal theory is irreducible]
\label{c04:thm:minimal-irreducible}
The minimal theory is irreducible: no layer of the hierarchy
\eqref{c04:eq:minimal} can be removed without losing derived content, and no further primitive
exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Irreducibility has two parts. \emph{Primitives:} by Theorem~\ref{c02:thm:minimal-foundation} of
Chapter~\ref{c02:chap:eta}, the minimal primitive set is $\{\text{Difference},\eta\}$; by
Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:difference-first}, no deeper notion exists; by
Theorem~\ref{c03:thm:derived-not-primitive} of Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization}, Actualization
is derived, not primitive. \emph{Layers:} the foundation stress test \cite{c04:QG292}
establishes that removing Difference collapses all layers, and the hierarchy-necessity audit
\cite{c04:QG293} that removing Actualization destroys the count-producing process.
The minimal-theory audit \cite{c04:QG294} confirms that the four layers of \eqref{c04:eq:minimal}
are exactly what is required, with zero additional inputs. Hence the minimal theory is
irreducible.
\end{proof}

The canonical architecture is therefore the minimal hierarchy \eqref{c04:eq:minimal}:
Difference → Actualization → Inevitable Spectrum → Physics, with primitives
$\{\text{Difference},\eta\}$, Actualization derived, the spectrum inevitable, and physics
emergent --- the referee-ready structure of MONO007 \cite{c04:MONO007}. Completeness is
understood, consistent with MONO007 \cite{c04:MONO007}, in the qualified sense: every
\emph{observable} is derived from the minimal hierarchy, while the standard-model
\emph{Lagrangian} and its dynamics are \emph{hosted} --- a boundary, not a derivation gap;
the minimal theory is irreducible as a derivation structure, not as a derivation of every
aspect of the hosted dynamics.

\section{Consequences}
\label{c04:sec:closure-consequences}

\begin{theorem}[Closure and minimality are consistent]
\label{c04:thm:closure-minimality}
The Closure Principle and the minimal theory are mutually consistent: the closure fixed
point $N=96$ is the output of the minimal hierarchy, and the minimal hierarchy requires the
closure to generate the spectrum. The reduction program thereby closes at the minimal
hierarchy \eqref{c04:eq:minimal}, with all intermediate layers of the fuller hierarchy
derivable from it and none actually required as an input.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The closure fixed point $N=96$ is the output of the actualization process
(Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-holds}), a layer of the minimal hierarchy; conversely, the
spectrum layer is the eigenspectrum of the converged network (Chapter~\ref{c03:chap:actualization},
Corollary~\ref{c03:cor:spectrum-eigenspectrum}), so the minimal hierarchy requires the closure
to produce the spectrum. The minimal-theory audit \cite{c04:QG294} classifies the compressed
layers as DERIVABLE (5 DERIVABLE / 0 ACTUALLY REQUIRED), with re-derivation using only the
minimal hierarchy.
\end{proof}

In the canonical architecture, Difference anchors the hierarchy (the root,
Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:difference-first}), the closure bounds it (the fixed point,
Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-holds}), and the minimal theory completes it (the irreducible
derivation structure, Theorems~\ref{c04:thm:minimal-hierarchy} and
\ref{c04:thm:minimal-irreducible}); the three roles are distinct and jointly exhaustive of
the foundational structure.

\section{Summary}
\label{c04:sec:closure-summary}

This chapter has established the foundational closure of the theory. The boundary of the
theory is the fixed point of Difference's own dynamics, closing at $N=96$
(Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-holds}); the Closure Principle characterizes but does not
derive the primitive, and the boundary is derived, not primitive
(Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:boundary-is-fixed}). Self-consistency and individuation presuppose
Difference (Theorems~\ref{c04:thm:self-consistency-presupposes} and
\ref{c04:thm:individuation}), Difference anchors the hierarchy as its self-referential first
concept (Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:difference-first}), and the minimal hierarchy is complete and
irreducible (Theorems~\ref{c04:thm:minimal-hierarchy} and \ref{c04:thm:minimal-irreducible}),
so closure and minimality are mutually consistent (Theorem~\ref{c04:thm:closure-minimality}).

The foundation of The Actualization Theory is therefore complete: the primitive Difference,
anchored by the Difference Principle; the closure that bounds its dynamics at $N=96$; and
the minimal theory that compresses the entire derivation structure to the irreducible
hierarchy Difference → Actualization → Spectrum → Physics. The following chapter derives,
from this foundation, the inevitable spectrum itself.
