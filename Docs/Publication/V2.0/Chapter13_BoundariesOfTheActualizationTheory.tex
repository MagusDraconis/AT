\chapter{Boundaries of The Actualization Theory}
\label{chap:boundaries}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:boundaries-introduction}

The preceding chapters derived the physics of The Actualization Theory: the foundation, the
spectrum, quantum mechanics, gravity, the standard model, and cosmology. This chapter is the
Boundary Layer of the monograph. Its purpose is threefold: to separate \emph{ontological
boundaries} from \emph{physics derivation}; to document the limits of the current framework
honestly; and to demonstrate that the boundaries are \emph{not derivation failures} ---
they are the explicit limits beyond which the framework does not claim to derive.

The boundaries documented here are: Difference as an ontological boundary \cite{QG278}; the
status of the tensor face $\psi$ \cite{QG286}; the numerical boundary $\pi$ \cite{QG291};
the Bekenstein $2\pi$ boundary \cite{QG185,QG196}; and the hosted standard-model dynamics
\cite{QG242,QG245}. The chapter also draws the essential distinction between \emph{derived
physics} and \emph{boundary questions} \cite{QG299}, and states explicitly what the theory
does not claim \cite{MONO007}.

Crucially, following VALID001 \cite{VALID001}, \emph{experimental validation is NOT treated
as a boundary}: the quantities $S,T,U$, the electron g-2 $a_e$, and the Majorana character
$0\nu\beta\beta$ are derived predictions awaiting experiment, not open derivation issues.
They are not listed as derivation gaps. This chapter uses only accepted canonical results
and introduces no new physics, no speculation, and \emph{no new boundaries}.

\section{The Nature of Scientific Boundaries}
\label{sec:nature-boundaries}

\begin{definition}[Scientific boundary]
\label{def:scientific-boundary}
A \emph{scientific boundary} is a limit of a framework beyond which the framework does not
claim to derive: either an irreducible primitive (an ontological boundary), a numerical
constant not derivable inside the framework (a numerical boundary), or a hosted structure
(an external component carried without derivation).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Boundaries are not failures]
\label{lem:boundaries-not-failures}
A documented boundary is not a derivation failure: it is the explicit, honest limit of the
framework, disclosed so that no claim exceeds what is established.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A framework is characterized as much by what it does not claim as by what it derives. The
disclosure of a boundary is the statement "this framework does not derive X"; it is a
necessary part of a referee-safe presentation \cite{MONO007}. The post-frontier audit
\cite{QG299} classifies the remaining items as open, partial, boundary, or methodology,
with the boundaries explicitly distinguished from the open derivation items. Hence a
boundary is an honest limit, not a failure of the derivation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Boundary vs validation]
Following VALID001 \cite{VALID001}, a distinction is drawn between a \emph{boundary} (a
limit of derivation) and an \emph{experimental-validation item} (a derived prediction
awaiting experiment). The derived quantities $S,T,U$, $a_e$, and $0\nu\beta\beta$ are
validation items, not boundaries: their derivations are complete, and their experimental
confirmation is the open task.
\end{remark}

\section{Difference as an Ontological Boundary}
\label{sec:difference-boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Difference is the ontological boundary]
\label{thm:difference-ontological}
Difference is the ontological boundary of the theory: the irreducible primitive whose
count-conservation law is its definitional identity, and which no deeper notion explains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental-boundary audit \cite{QG278} establishes that Difference is the first
concept: every attempt to reduce it reintroduces it (two distinct things use distinct =
difference; a boundary is where things differ; a transition is a before/after difference).
By Chapter~\ref{chap:difference} (Theorem~\ref{thm:fundamental-boundary}), Difference is the
fundamental boundary of the theory. As an ontological boundary, it is not a derivation
failure but the primitive limit of the framework: the theory begins here.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Difference is the primitive boundary]
\label{cor:difference-primitive-boundary}
Difference is identified as the primitive boundary of the theory: it is the irreducible
starting point, not an unexplained gap in the derivation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:difference-ontological}, Difference is the irreducible first concept.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:boundaries-not-failures}, an ontological boundary is an honest primitive
limit. Hence Difference is the primitive boundary of the theory.
\end{proof}

\section{The Status of \texorpdfstring{$\psi$}{psi}}
\label{sec:psi-status}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$\psi$}{psi} is the tensor face of Difference]
\label{thm:psi-face}
The tensor field $\psi$ is the tensor face of Difference: the traceless part of the
trace/traceless decomposition of the one Difference, read against the tensor reference
$\eta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Difference duality \cite{QG286} establishes that $\rho$ and $\psi$ are the trace and
traceless faces of the one Difference (Chapter~\ref{chap:difference},
Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}); the tensor face is read against the reference $\eta$
(Chapter~\ref{chap:eta}, Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor-read}). Hence $\psi$ is the tensor face of
Difference --- not an independent primitive.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$\psi$}{psi} has unresolved ontological status]
\label{cor:psi-ontological}
The ontological status of $\psi$ is unresolved: it is established as the tensor face of
Difference, but the question of its deepest ontological nature --- whether the tensor
content is ultimately irreducible to the scalar count --- remains a boundary question, not a
derivation gap.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:psi-face}, $\psi$ is the tensor face of Difference. The post-frontier
audit \cite{QG299} classifies the $\psi$ question as a boundary item: the tensor face
exists and its observables are derived, but its ultimate ontological status is a boundary
question. Consistent with MONO007 \cite{MONO007}, the monograph presents $\psi$ as a face
of Difference with unresolved ontological status, never as a derivation failure.
\end{proof}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} Boundary}
\label{sec:pi-boundary}

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} as a numerical boundary]
\label{def:pi-boundary}
\emph{$\pi$} is the universal mathematical constant: the geometric ratio of the circle. In
the canonical architecture it is a \emph{numerical boundary} --- a constant not derivable
inside the framework --- and not a primitive.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} is redundant as a theory input]
\label{thm:pi-redundant}
$\pi$ is redundant as a theory input: no derived prediction of the theory uses $\pi$ as an
input, and where it appears it is an imported numerical factor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The framework-necessity audit \cite{QG291} establishes the classification: every derived
observable --- masses, couplings, mixings, cosmological fractions, spectral indices,
acoustic ratios, and the pre-registered predictions --- is a pure ratio of D96 spectral
constants together with the calibration scale. $\pi$ appears only where an imported quantum
factor is required, most notably in the Bekenstein coefficient. Hence $\pi$ is redundant as
a theory input --- a numerical boundary, not a primitive.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} is a boundary, not a primitive]
\label{cor:pi-boundary}
$\pi$ is a numerical boundary of the framework: it is not derivable inside the framework,
it is not used as a derived-prediction input, and it is not a primitive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:pi-redundant}, $\pi$ is not a derived-prediction input; by
Definition~\ref{def:pi-boundary}, it is a numerical constant not derivable inside the
framework. Hence $\pi$ is a numerical boundary, distinct from the primitive reference
$\eta$ (Chapter~\ref{chap:eta}, Theorem~\ref{thm:eta-primitive-pi-boundary}).
\end{proof}

\section{The Bekenstein \texorpdfstring{$2\pi$}{2pi} Boundary}
\label{sec:bekenstein}

\begin{theorem}[The Bekenstein $1/4$ coefficient requires the imported $2\pi$ factor]
\label{thm:bekenstein-2pi}
The exact Bekenstein coefficient $S = A/4$ is a boundary: the structure
$S \propto A$, $M \propto R$, $T \propto 1/R$ is derived, but the exact $1/4$ coefficient
requires the imported quantum factor $T = \kappa/(2\pi)$, which the framework cannot produce
internally.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Bekenstein origin \cite{QG185} derives the structure ($S \propto A$, $M \propto R$,
$T \propto 1/R$) and the deficit first-law gives $S = A/(8\pi)$, not $1/4$; the exact $1/4$
requires the $2\pi$ quantum factor $T = \kappa/(2\pi)$, absent in the framework. The
impossibility proof \cite{QG196} confirms that the exact $1/4$ cannot be derived from the
framework without importing $\pi$. Hence the Bekenstein $1/4$ coefficient is explicitly tied
to the imported $2\pi$ factor, and is a documented boundary, not a derivation claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Bekenstein boundary is explicitly referenced]
\label{cor:bekenstein-referenced}
The Bekenstein $2\pi$ boundary is explicitly referenced to the imported quantum factor and
is not presented as a derivation of the theory.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:bekenstein-2pi}, the exact coefficient requires the imported $2\pi$
factor. Consistent with MONO007 \cite{MONO007} and Chapter~\ref{chap:gravity}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bekenstein-boundary}), the boundary is referenced, not claimed.
\end{proof}

\section{Hosted Standard Model Dynamics}
\label{sec:hosted-sm}

\begin{theorem}[The SM Lagrangian is hosted, not derived]
\label{thm:sm-hosted}
The standard-model \emph{Lagrangian} and its dynamics are hosted: the framework derives the
observables (masses, couplings, mixings), but carries the Lagrangian form as an external
structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The SM-dynamics audits \cite{QG242,QG245} establish the classification: the gauge
\emph{symmetry} is derived, but the gauge \emph{dynamics} (the Lagrangian, vertices,
propagators) is hosted; the SM dynamics is not complete as a derivation. The derived
observables --- the fermion sectors, couplings, mixings, weak scale, and precision
predictions (Chapter~\ref{chap:sm}) --- are reads of the D96 moments. Hence the SM
dynamics is hosted, while the observables are derived.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hosted dynamics vs derived observables]
\label{cor:hosted-vs-derived}
A distinction is drawn between the hosted SM dynamics (the Lagrangian and its structure)
and the derived SM observables (the values read from the D96 moments): the former is a
boundary, the latter is derived physics.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sm-hosted}, the dynamics is hosted and the observables are derived.
Hence the monograph distinguishes the hosted structure (a boundary) from the derived values
(established physics), presenting each in its correct role.
\end{proof}

\section{Derived Physics vs Boundary Questions}
\label{sec:derived-vs-boundary}

\begin{theorem}[The classification of remaining items]
\label{thm:classification}
The remaining items of the framework classify into derived physics, experimental
validation, and boundaries: the derived physics is established; the validation items
($S,T,U$, $a_e$, $0\nu\beta\beta$) are derived predictions awaiting experiment; the
boundaries are the ontological, numerical, and hosted limits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The post-frontier audit \cite{QG299} classifies the remaining items; VALID001
\cite{VALID001} establishes that $S,T,U$, $a_e$, and $0\nu\beta\beta$ are \emph{derived
predictions} (status A) belonging to the experimental-validation category, with no missing
derivation steps. The boundaries are those documented in this chapter. Hence the remaining
items partition into derived physics (established), validation (derived, awaiting
experiment), and boundaries (the limits).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No validation item is an open derivation issue]
\label{cor:no-open-validation}
The quantities $S,T,U$, the electron g-2 $a_e$, and the Majorana character $0\nu\beta\beta$
are not listed as open derivation issues: their derivations are complete, and their
experimental validation is the open task.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} and VALID001 \cite{VALID001}, all three quantities are
status A (derived prediction exists) with no missing derivation steps, classified under
experimental validation. Hence they are validation items, not open derivation issues, and
not boundaries.
\end{proof}

\section{What Is Not Claimed}
\label{sec:not-claimed}

\begin{theorem}[The framework's non-claims]
\label{thm:non-claims}
The framework does not claim: (i) a derivation of the standard-model Lagrangian (hosted);
(ii) a derivation of the exact Bekenstein $1/4$ coefficient (requires the imported $2\pi$
factor); (iii) a resolution of the ultimate ontological status of $\psi$; (iv) any result
beyond the accepted derivations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The SM dynamics is hosted \cite{QG242,QG245} (Theorem~\ref{thm:sm-hosted}). (ii) The
Bekenstein $1/4$ coefficient requires the imported $2\pi$ factor \cite{QG185,QG196}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bekenstein-2pi}). (iii) The ontological status of $\psi$ is unresolved
(Corollary~\ref{cor:psi-ontological}). (iv) The monograph is assembly-only, using accepted
results and introducing no new physics and no speculation \cite{MONO007}. Hence the
non-claims are explicit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The claims are exactly the derivations]
\label{cor:claims-are-derivations}
The framework claims exactly what it derives: the physics observables established in the
preceding chapters, with the boundaries disclosed and the validation items awaiting
experiment.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:non-claims}, the non-claims are explicit; by the derivation chapters,
the claimed content is the derived physics. Hence the claims equal the derivations, no more
and no less, consistent with the referee-safe architecture \cite{MONO007}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Referee-safe wording]
Consistent with MONO007 \cite{MONO007}, every statement in this chapter is worded to make
the boundaries explicit without presenting them as failures, and to keep the experimental
validation items separate from the boundaries. No claim here exceeds the accepted results.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences for Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

\begin{theorem}[The boundaries delimit the future work]
\label{thm:future-work}
The boundaries delimit the future work of the framework: the experimental validation of the
derived predictions, the resolution of the ontological status of $\psi$, and the question
of whether the imported $2\pi$ factor can be internalized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The derived predictions ($S,T,U$, $a_e$, $0\nu\beta\beta$) await experimental validation
\cite{VALID001}; the ontological status of $\psi$ is a boundary question
(Corollary~\ref{cor:psi-ontological}); the Bekenstein $2\pi$ factor is an imported boundary
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bekenstein-2pi}). These define the honest future work: the validation
items are experimental tasks, the ontological and numerical boundaries are the limits the
framework does not claim to cross.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Boundaries are not blockers]
\label{cor:boundaries-not-blockers}
The boundaries are not blockers to the established physics: the derived content of the
theory is complete within its scope, and the boundaries mark where the framework honestly
stops.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:future-work}, the boundaries delimit the future work; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundaries-not-failures}, they are not failures. The derived physics is
complete within the canonical hierarchy (Chapter~\ref{chap:cosmology},
Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-cosmology}); the boundaries are the honest limits. Hence the
boundaries are not blockers but the framework's declared edge.
\end{proof}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:boundaries-summary}

This chapter has documented the boundaries of the theory. We have proved:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The nature of boundaries} (Lemma~\ref{lem:boundaries-not-failures}):
boundaries are honest limits, not derivation failures;
\item \textbf{Difference as an ontological boundary} (Theorem~\ref{thm:difference-ontological},
Corollary~\ref{cor:difference-primitive-boundary}): Difference is the irreducible primitive
boundary;
\item \textbf{The status of $\psi$} (Theorem~\ref{thm:psi-face},
Corollary~\ref{cor:psi-ontological}): $\psi$ is the tensor face of Difference with
unresolved ontological status;
\item \textbf{The $\pi$ boundary} (Theorem~\ref{thm:pi-redundant},
Corollary~\ref{cor:pi-boundary}): $\pi$ is a numerical boundary, redundant as a theory
input;
\item \textbf{The Bekenstein $2\pi$ boundary} (Theorem~\ref{thm:bekenstein-2pi},
Corollary~\ref{cor:bekenstein-referenced}): the $1/4$ coefficient is explicitly tied to the
imported $2\pi$ factor;
\item \textbf{Hosted SM dynamics} (Theorem~\ref{thm:sm-hosted},
Corollary~\ref{cor:hosted-vs-derived}): the SM Lagrangian is hosted, the observables
derived;
\item \textbf{Derived vs boundary} (Theorem~\ref{thm:classification},
Corollary~\ref{cor:no-open-validation}): the remaining items classify into derived,
validation, and boundary --- with no validation item an open derivation issue;
\item \textbf{Non-claims} (Theorem~\ref{thm:non-claims},
Corollary~\ref{cor:claims-are-derivations}): the framework claims exactly its derivations;
\item \textbf{Future work} (Theorem~\ref{thm:future-work},
Corollary~\ref{cor:boundaries-not-blockers}): the boundaries delimit the honest future
work, and are not blockers.
\end{enumerate}

The boundaries of The Actualization Theory are therefore: Difference (the ontological
boundary and primitive), $\psi$ (the tensor face with unresolved ontological status),
$\pi$ (the numerical boundary), the Bekenstein $2\pi$ factor (the imported quantum
boundary), and the hosted standard-model dynamics --- each an explicit limit, none a
derivation failure. The derived physics of the theory is complete within its scope; the
experimental validation of the derived predictions is the open task; and the boundaries mark
where the framework honestly stops. This closes the Boundary Layer of the monograph; the
following chapter documents the frontier and the falsification paths.
