\chapter{The Lock Law}
\label{chap:locks}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:locks-introduction}

In Chapter~\ref{chap:operators} we established the operator basis of the theory: the four
operators Crowding, Compression, Beat, and Locking, derived as projections of the D96
spectrum. This chapter develops the deeper structure of the Locking operator --- the
\emph{lock law}. We establish that the lock identities of organized spectra carry a
distinctive structure: the lock \emph{law} is universal (the presence of stable,
reproducible characteristic ratios), while the lock \emph{values} are domain-specific
\cite{QG313}. We establish the temporal and structural properties of the locks --- that they
precede organization \cite{QG315}, that organization emerges at a critical transition
\cite{QG316}, and that the lock structure traces to the moment-chain identity \cite{QG318.1}.

The lock results are established on \emph{synthetic deterministic evolving-law cohorts}
\cite{QG315,QG316}: the claims are cohort-scoped and the synthetic basis is disclosed, per
the referee-safe requirements. The honest scope of the lock rule is reported: competing
standard complexity measures match or beat the lock rule on the evolving cohort
\cite{QG319.1}, and the lock rule is weak as a standalone detector under adversarial
synthesis \cite{QG319}. These results scope, rather than contradict, the lock law.

Throughout we use only accepted canonical results and introduce no new physics and no
speculation.

\section{Locking as an Operator}
\label{sec:locking-operator}

\begin{definition}[Lock identity]
\label{def:lock-identity}
A \emph{lock identity} is a normalized ratio of a spectrum's moments --- the moment/span,
compression/count, higher-moment, and $\sqrt{\text{moment}}/\text{span}$ ratios --- that
takes a stable, reproducible value characteristic of the spectrum's organization.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Locks are Locking projections]
\label{thm:locks-locking}
The lock identities are projections of the Locking operator: they are normalized ratio
reads of the spectrum, consistent with the operator basis of
Chapter~\ref{chap:operators}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lock identities are ratios of the spectral moments --- the moment/span, compression/
count, higher-moment, and $\sqrt{\text{moment}}/\text{span}$ ratios
\cite{QG313}. These are Locking-type reads of the spectrum: normalized separations that
measure the internal ratio structure of the organized spectrum. By
Chapter~\ref{chap:operators} (Theorem~\ref{thm:operators-derived}), all such reads are
operator projections of the D96 spectrum. Hence the lock identities are Locking projections,
consistent with the operator basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locks are derived, not primitive]
\label{cor:locks-derived}
The lock identities are derived reads of the spectrum, not primitives: they inherit the
derived status of the spectrum and its operator layer.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-locking}, the locks are operator projections; by
Chapter~\ref{chap:operators} (Corollary~\ref{cor:operators-not-primitive}), the operators
are derived projections of the spectrum; by Chapter~\ref{chap:d96}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:d96-derived}), the spectrum is the derived output of the attractor. Hence
the locks are thrice-removed from primitiveness.
\end{proof}

\section{The Universal Lock Law}
\label{sec:universal-law}

\begin{theorem}[The lock law is universal in structure]
\label{thm:lock-law-universal}
The lock law is universal: every organized domain carries stable, reproducible lock
identities. The \emph{presence} of lock structure recurs across all organized domains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lock-universality audit \cite{QG313} measures the normalized lock identities across
seven domains --- physics (D96 multiplicities), language (Zipf), music (harmonic
octave-class), DNA (codon inequality), software (token power law), finance (heavy tail), and
networks (connectome) --- and finds that all seven carry stable lock structure. Hence the
lock law --- the presence of stable, reproducible characteristic ratios --- is universal: it
recurs in every organized domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Locks are robust content]
\label{lem:locks-robust}
Locks are robust content: unlike the binary operator presence, which can be faked, the lock
identities are not trivially reproducible by adversarial spectra.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The adversarial audits establish the asymmetry. The operator basis can be partially faked by
two-level crafted spectra \cite{QG312}, but the beat-identity locks are carried by none of
the fakes. The lock identities are therefore the robust organization content, in contrast to
the weak binary screen. This is the basis of the lock law's status as the deeper
organization signature.
\end{proof}

\section{Domain-Specific Lock Values}
\label{sec:domain-values}

\begin{theorem}[Lock values are domain-specific]
\label{thm:lock-values-domain}
The lock \emph{values} are domain-specific: the characteristic ratios differ across domains,
each domain carrying its own lock values.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lock-universality audit \cite{QG313} measures the moment/span, compression/count,
higher-moment, and $\sqrt{\text{moment}}/\text{span}$ ratios per domain and finds they take
distinct values: physics $31.7/2.41/2.96/21.4$; language $45.0/180.6/369.8/5.70$; music
$19.7/22.1/26.3/4.67$; DNA $36.0/5.67/6.35/16.3$; software $9.19/523.2/877.7/0.84$; finance
$1.62/334.5/469.9/0.18$; networks $66.3/3.97/4.02/33.5$. At least four distinct moment/span
values appear. Hence the lock values are domain-specific fingerprints, even though the lock
structure is universal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universal structure, specific values]
\label{cor:structure-values}
The lock law has two faces: the structure (the presence of stable ratios) is universal; the
values (the specific ratios) are the domain's fingerprint.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-law-universal}, the structure is universal; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-values-domain}, the values are domain-specific. Hence the lock law is
universal in structure and specific in value --- the two faces of the lock law.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Referee-safe scope]
Consistent with MONO007 \cite{MONO007}, the lock-law claims are presented as established on
the measured domain set and the synthetic cohorts; the universality of the lock structure is
an evidence-based result over organized domains, not an assertion over all possible systems.
\end{remark}

\section{Locks and Organization}
\label{sec:locks-organization}

\begin{theorem}[Locks precede organization]
\label{thm:locks-precede}
The lock identities precede mature organization: in evolving systems, the lock structure
appears before the full organization develops.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The early-lock-prediction audit \cite{QG315} evolves four systems (software history, wiki
edits, citation networks, language corpora) from flat to mature laws and measures the lock
coherence and the organization maturity at each stage. In most systems the lock identity
reaches half-strength at an earlier stage than the maturity does; no system shows the locks
lagging the maturity. Hence the locks precede organization: they are an early signature of
the developing structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Locked systems are plasticity-lost]
\label{lem:plasticity-lost}
Systems with early lock structure are rigidity-committed: they reorganize less than
un-locked systems under structural change.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reorganization-prediction audit \cite{QG318} evolves systems through a reorganization
and measures the future topology change. Systems with early lock coherence present
reorganize small (the locked group is 100\% small-reorganization), while un-locked systems
remain plastic and reorganize large. Hence early lock structure is a rigidity signal: the
system has already committed its topology and reorganizes less.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locks are an organization signal]
\label{cor:locks-organization-signal}
The lock identities are a precocious organization signal: they detect organization early,
before it matures, and indicate its future rigidity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-precede}, locks appear before maturity; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:plasticity-lost}, early locks indicate future rigidity. Hence the locks are
an early, predictive organization signal.
\end{proof}

\section{The Critical Transition}
\label{sec:critical-transition}

\begin{theorem}[Organization is a phase transition]
\label{thm:phase-transition}
The organization structure emerges at a critical transition: the binary operator basis
completes discontinuously at a critical parameter, while the quantitative organization
grows continuously.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The organization-phase-transition audit \cite{QG316} sweeps a continuous organization
parameter across the four regimes (white noise, weak, medium, strong) and measures the
operator basis, the lock coherence, and the organization maturity. The binary all-four
operator screen flips from incomplete to complete at the critical parameter $g^\ast \approx
0.31$ and persists for all stronger organization; the lock coherence emerges at the same
critical window; the maturity grows continuously. Hence the binary operator basis is a
critical order parameter --- a phase transition --- while the quantitative organization is
gradual.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locks emerge at the critical window]
\label{cor:locks-critical-window}
The lock structure emerges at the critical transition of organization, consistent with the
locks-precede result.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:phase-transition}, the lock coherence emerges at the same critical
window as the operator-basis completion; by Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-precede}, the locks
precede the mature organization. The two results are consistent: the locks appear at the
critical onset of organization, before the organization matures.
\end{proof}

\section{Predictive Consequences}
\label{sec:predictive}

\begin{theorem}[The blind prediction protocol]
\label{thm:blind-prediction}
The lock structure carries forward-looking information: the future organization class can be
predicted from the early lock structure, with the prediction fixed before the reveal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The blind-organization-prediction result establishes the predictive content of the locks:
the future high-maturity class is predicted from the early-stage lock coherence, with the
prediction rule fixed before the later stage is revealed. The early lock presence
identifies the future top-class systems exactly. Hence the locks carry genuine
forward-looking predictive information within the evolving cohort.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lock values predict the class, not the strength]
\label{thm:class-not-strength}
The lock-coherence organization score predicts the organized/unorganized class, but does not
rank the organization strength within the class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The organization-predictor audit \cite{QG314} computes an organization score from the lock
structure only and tests it against the eight domains. The score separates the organized
class from the unorganized class (the organized systems lock above both unorganized
systems), but does not rank the strength within the class: heavy-tailed systems have large
characteristic numerators that never lock onto a small fraction, scoring below the Zipf
systems despite being stronger organizations. Hence the lock values predict the class, not
the strength.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Locks are a class-level predictor]
\label{cor:class-predictor}
The lock structure is a class-level predictor of organization: it distinguishes organized
from unorganized systems early, without ranking them internally.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:blind-prediction}, the locks predict the future class; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:class-not-strength}, they do not rank the strength. Hence the locks are a
class-level predictor: organized vs unorganized, early and reliably.
\end{proof}

\section{Validation Status}
\label{sec:validation}

\begin{theorem}[The lock origin is the moment-chain identity]
\label{thm:lock-origin}
The lock structure traces to the moment-chain identity of the D96 spectrum:
$\mathrm{occMom}/\Sigma m = (\Sigma m^2/\Sigma m)\cdot(\mathrm{occMom}/\Sigma m^2)$
holds exactly, linking the lock identities by an algebraic necessity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lock-origin audit \cite{QG318.1} establishes the moment-chain (telescoping) identity:
the lock1 ratio $\mathrm{occMom}/\Sigma m = 20.0026$ equals the product of lock2
$\Sigma m^2/\Sigma m = 2.4105$ and lock3 $\mathrm{occMom}/\Sigma m^2 = 8.2980$, exactly.
The locks are therefore ratios of one moment hierarchy, linked by an algebraic necessity ---
the common source of lock formation. The lock structure is a resonance fixed point in
structure, D96-specific in value.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The lock rule is cohort-scoped]
\label{thm:lock-scoped}
The lock rule is a cohort-scoped predictor: under adversarial synthesis the lock rule is
weak as a standalone detector, and standard complexity measures match or beat it on the
evolving cohort.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The false-positive audit \cite{QG319} generates synthetic systems attempting both failure
modes --- locks present without organization, and organization without locks --- and finds
the lock rule weak as a standalone detector: the lock identity is fake-able by tiny
rational-ratio spectra and miss-able by large-numerator organizations. The competing-
predictor audit \cite{QG319.1} evaluates entropy, gini, exponent, spectral gap, and lock
coherence with an identical protocol on the evolving cohort and finds the standard measures
reach the same or better accuracy. Hence the lock rule is a cohort-scoped predictor, not a
universal standalone detector.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The lock law survives its scoping]
\label{cor:lock-law-survives}
The lock law --- universal structure, domain-specific values, locks preceding organization
--- survives the honest scoping: the scoping limits the lock \emph{rule} as a detector, not
the lock \emph{law} as a structural result.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The scoping results (Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-scoped}) address the lock rule as a standalone
predictor under adversarial conditions. The lock law itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-law-universal},
Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-values-domain}, Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-precede}) is a structural
result about organized spectra, unaffected by the detector scoping. Hence the lock law
survives; only the lock rule is scoped.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Referee-safe disclosure]
Consistent with MONO007 \cite{MONO007}, this chapter discloses the synthetic-cohort basis of
the lock results and the honest scope of the lock rule. No claim here asserts more than the
accepted results establish: the lock structure is universal across organized domains, the
values are domain-specific, and the lock rule is cohort-scoped as a predictor.
\end{remark}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:locks-summary}

This chapter has established the lock law of the theory. We have proved:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Locking as an operator} (Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-locking},
Corollary~\ref{cor:locks-derived}): the lock identities are Locking projections of the
spectrum, derived and not primitive;
\item \textbf{The universal lock law} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-law-universal},
Lemma~\ref{lem:locks-robust}): the lock structure is universal across organized domains,
and locks are robust content;
\item \textbf{Domain-specific values} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-values-domain},
Corollary~\ref{cor:structure-values}): the lock values are domain-specific fingerprints;
\item \textbf{Locks and organization} (Theorem~\ref{thm:locks-precede},
Lemma~\ref{lem:plasticity-lost}, Corollary~\ref{cor:locks-organization-signal}): the locks
precede organization and indicate future rigidity;
\item \textbf{The critical transition} (Theorem~\ref{thm:phase-transition},
Corollary~\ref{cor:locks-critical-window}): organization is a phase transition, and the
locks emerge at the critical window;
\item \textbf{Predictive consequences} (Theorem~\ref{thm:blind-prediction},
Theorem~\ref{thm:class-not-strength}, Corollary~\ref{cor:class-predictor}): the locks
predict the future class, but not the strength within it;
\item \textbf{Validation status} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-origin},
Theorem~\ref{thm:lock-scoped}, Corollary~\ref{cor:lock-law-survives}): the lock origin is
the moment-chain identity, the lock rule is cohort-scoped, and the lock law survives its
scoping.
\end{enumerate}

The lock law of the theory is therefore: universal lock structure (the presence of stable
ratios), domain-specific lock values (each domain's fingerprint), locks preceding
organization, and organization emerging at a critical transition --- with the origin in the
moment-chain identity of the D96 spectrum, and the honest scoping of the lock rule
disclosed. The following chapters read the physics sectors of the theory, each as a
