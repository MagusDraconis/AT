# THE Q-MODEL (AT) — Zenodo Abstract

THE Q-MODEL (AT) is a theory of structure and content that compresses observable physics
to four primitives — the individuation principle $Q$, Random Actualization, the scale
triad $(\ell,\tau,\hbar)$, and a single nonlinearity parameter $M^2$ — and holds that
*structure is derivable while content is realized*. Its central claim is the
structure/content split: the *form* of observable structure (topology, symmetry,
distribution shape, lower bounds) is provable from the primitives, whereas the *content*
(masses, couplings, multiplicities, angles) is a contingent draw, classified rather than
derived.

The theory derives $U(1)$ as the symmetry of phase, spatial dimensionality $3{+}1$ from
complexity maximization, and the Schr\"odinger equation from norm conservation; the
abundance law's log-normal form follows from a multiplicative cascade. It classifies the
Koide relation ($Q=2/3$) and the $SU(2)/SU(3)$ structure as real-underived, and the
couplings, masses, and color count as drawn. The continuum program verifies two controlled
limits — the graph Laplacian $L_Q \to -\nabla^2$ and the causal-set BDG operator $\to
\square$ — and closes the metric origin via the causal order, the conformal class
(Malament's theorem), and the counting-measure conformal factor.

All verified claims are backed by an executable xUnit program: **15 test files, 47 tests,
all passing** (the Einstein recovery is noted partial). Remaining open issues are the
native metric-to-operator coupling (G4), a native derivation of the BDG action, and a
